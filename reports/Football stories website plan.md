# Build Football Stories Around Access, Not Prose

Frankly: a text-first "football stories" website started solo in Singapore in late 2026 will not become a business inside two years, and the closest documented comparable (a football-writing Substack that reached about 50 paying subscribers after 700 posts and quit) says it is marginal even at three. It is, however, a worthwhile long-horizon project with real upside if you reshape it around three things the evidence rewards: a rigid, repeatable story unit modelled on The Morning Founder (one subject, one decision, 1,200–1,800 words, researched from the record, numbered and illustrated, delivered by email first); a differentiating wedge that no global network serves, namely Singapore and Malaysia Cup-era history, Southeast Asian players and Asian-timezone fan culture, with global legends used only as discovery bait; and an owned audience (email list from day one) fed by native social formats rather than link posts, because Google referrals to publishers fell a third in 2025 and Facebook link reach has collapsed. The Morning Founder itself is not the Facebook storytelling page the brief assumed; it is a performance marketer's free daily beehiiv newsletter with a week-delayed website, per-issue sponsorship and a 260-story research backlog, and every element of that formula transfers to football. Globally, football storytelling is crowded at every tier (The Athletic's newsletters at 6M, Tifo at 1.82M YouTube subscribers, These Football Times with a 73k list), but Southeast Asia has no dedicated storytelling player at all; the biggest local "competitor" is a fan podcast averaging about 500 plays an episode. The recommended build is Astro plus GitHub Actions plus Pages CMS in a fresh repository (your current repo is an automated dashboard that commits several times a day), a free-tier newsletter tool, cookieless analytics, a year-one budget between US$11 and roughly US$400, a fortnightly deep story plus weekly short formats at six to eight hours a week, and a 12-month kill-or-continue checkpoint judged on list size and read-depth, not revenue.

## The Morning Founder is a direct-response newsletter, not a social page

The premise needs correcting before it can be copied. The Morning Founder describes itself as "Southeast Asian business history, researched from the record and illustrated", written by Joel Chue, "based in Singapore and writing daily since 2024", with each issue profiling "one company and one decision, with no news roundups" ([The Morning Founder](https://www.themorningfounder.com/)). Chue states on his About page that he is "not a journalist", has "about twenty years in performance marketing", built and sold an app business, and writes a research brief of "about ten to twenty pages for each story" focused on "decisions that looked wrong at the time" ([About](https://themorningfounder.com/about/)). His LinkedIn career is in user acquisition and media buying, including a CMO role at a Singapore digital-publishing company he left around August 2024 ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/joel-chue-aa698a51/)). No official Facebook or Instagram page for the publication could be found; the only social channel in use is Chue's personal LinkedIn, where posts "open with a founder story and close with a question for readers" ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/joel-chue-aa698a51/)). The growth engine is email plus LinkedIn plus, very likely given his background, paid acquisition, not viral Facebook shares.

The repeatable unit is unusually tight. **One company's story per issue, 1,200 to 1,800 words, seven mornings a week, "with a watercolour made for it"** ([homepage](https://www.themorningfounder.com/)), sent at **8am Singapore time on weekdays and 10am at weekends** ([Subscribe](https://themorningfounder.com/subscribe)). Each story is numbered as a "Plate" (Plate 075, Plate 120, Plate 148), tagged by country and category, labelled "Watercolour" and given a reading time, with headlines in the form "Vodien / Alvin Poh" or "Carousell / Quek Siu Rui" ([Archive](https://themorningfounder.com/stories)). The teasers are number-led hooks: a founder who "sold in 2017 for $30 million at 33", founders who "turned down US$100 million", a chili-sauce business "with no marketing spend" ([Archive](https://themorningfounder.com/stories)). The method is stated as policy: stories are "rebuilt from public material such as company filings, news reports, interviews, books, and archives", "each claim is checked twice against at least two sources", disagreements between sources are flagged in the text, and errors go on a dated corrections page ([Disclaimer](https://themorningfounder.com/disclaimer/); [Terms](https://themorningfounder.com/terms/)). The single commissioned illustration is a shrewd move for historical subjects: it gives the series a collectible identity and sidesteps photo rights entirely, which matters even more in football.

The website is deliberately subordinate to the email. **Stories appear on the site a week after subscribers read them**, and newer story pages say "the newsletter gets the complete text first" ([Archive](https://themorningfounder.com/stories); [NousQ story](https://themorningfounder.com/stories/nousq)). The site is seven pages: Home, Stories archive, About, Subscribe, Sponsor an issue, Terms, Disclaimer, plus a corrections page ([Disclaimer](https://themorningfounder.com/disclaimer/)). Email runs on beehiiv, confirmed by a beehiiv mirror and by the sponsor page showing subscriber count and 30-day open rate as a "Live figure from beehiiv" ([beehiiv](https://the-morning-founder.beehiiv.com/); [Advertise](https://themorningfounder.com/advertise)). Monetisation is the "Morning Brew for X" model: the newsletter is free ([Terms](https://themorningfounder.com/terms/)), and sponsors buy a **70-word "Primary" slot placed inside the story, or a 25-word logo-only "Frontispiece" under the header, one primary per issue, booked by emailing the dates wanted** ([Sponsor an issue](https://themorningfounder.com/advertise)). Sponsors buy "a labelled slot in an issue, not editorial control" ([Terms](https://themorningfounder.com/terms/)). The publisher is a Singapore company, ReallyCreativeIdeas.com Pte Ltd ([Terms](https://themorningfounder.com/terms/)).

Scale is the honest caveat. The only audience number anywhere is that the first story, about a Malaysian engineer who refunded every customer for 200 jars of mis-made sambal, drew about **4,000 readers** ([homepage](https://www.themorningfounder.com/)). The archive grew from 120 to **156 plates** between snapshots, with "roughly 260 more researched and waiting" ([Subscribe](https://themorningfounder.com/subscribe); [About](https://themorningfounder.com/about/)). No subscriber count, open rate, sponsorship price or press coverage surfaced in any search; the live beehiiv widget on the advertise page would show the list size if you open it in a browser. The transferable formula, then, is not "a page that went viral" but a system: a tight unit, a fixed daily slot, a deep researched backlog that guarantees cadence, a rights-free visual identity, an email-first funnel with the website as delayed archive, labelled per-issue sponsorship from day one, and an explicit "not a journalist, but two sources per claim" credibility stance. For a documented Southeast Asian newsletter growth playbook, BackScoop (Philippines, 10,000+ active subscribers) has published far more than Chue has ([Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/how-amanda-cua-built-backscoop-southeast-asias-go-to-tech-newsletter/)).

## Durable storytelling brands repeat one unit, then own the list

Across twelve storytelling brands in other niches, the pattern that holds is one rigid unit repeated for years, a subject-first voice with the creator out of frame, one borrowed platform at a time, and then an owned channel. Humans of New York began in November 2010 as a plan to photograph 10,000 New Yorkers; the quotes "became interviews, until it grew organically", reaching over **11 million Facebook likes by December 2014** and roughly 30 million cross-platform followers by 2025 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2014/12/05/how-ex-bond-traders-humans-of-new-york-became-a-viral-hit.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humans_of_New_York)). Brandon Stanton's own explanation is the most useful sentence in the whole corpus: "the more I removed myself from the story, the faster it has grown" ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/meet-the-human-behind-humans-of-new-york?amp=1)). His interviews run 45 to 60 minutes, one-on-one, opening with questions like "What are you most afraid of right now?" ([SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/inclusion-diversity/humans-new-york-blogger-shares-interviewing-listening-tips-di-conference)). Serialisation is the lever that turns followers into daily returners: the multi-part "Tanqueray" story raised over **$2.5 million against a $300,000 goal in about two weeks** ([AOL](https://www.aol.com/humans-york-raises-2-6m-213513504.html)).

The Singapore cases show the same mechanics at local scale. The Woke Salaryman's duo had been "experimenting with content prototypes for years" before launch; the breakout was a true family story about a father losing his job in the 1997 crisis, which went viral when PM Lee Hsien Loong shared it, and the page grew from over 170k Facebook followers in 2020 to a self-reported **500k-plus across Instagram and TikTok** today ([Goh Wei Choon LinkedIn](https://sg.linkedin.com/in/weichoon); [With Content](https://withcontent.co/the-woke-salaryman-personal-finance-content/); [TWS Substack About](https://thewokesalaryman.substack.com/about)). Our Grandfather Story began as a 2017 NTU assignment, had over 300k Facebook followers by about 2020 and roughly **444k YouTube subscribers by November 2025**, funded by branded work for Temasek, the National Heritage Board and Grab under a stated policy of turning down campaigns that "would not sit right with their audience" ([Techcoffeehouse](https://techcoffeehouse.com/2022/12/22/our-grandfather-story-it-all-started-from-a-school-assignment/amp/); [Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q64990230); [So This Is My Why](https://www.sothisismywhy.com/ng-kai-yuan-cofounder-our-grandfather-story/)). Mothership is now the **second most-used online news source in Singapore at 45% weekly reach**, ahead of the Straits Times online, and runs a Telegram channel of about 247k alongside Facebook and TikTok ([Reuters Institute DNR 2026](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/singapore); [Telegram](https://t.me/mothershipsg)). The cautionary local tale is Rice Media: branded-content-only, it retrenched five staff in late 2025 and was sold in March 2026 to a buyer whose deal let the founder "settle most of the liabilities" ([Yahoo News SG](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/news-website-rice-media-talks-120531403.html); [Vulcan Post](https://vulcanpost.com/910395/rice-media-acquisition/)).

Newsletter brands show how the first 10,000 are won: manually. Morning Brew's first ~10,000 came from student "Brew-bassadors" collecting emails by hand in lectures, and its "Share the Brew" referral programme is credited with about 30% of total subscribers on the way to a ~$75M sale ([Marketing Crafted](https://marketingcrafted.com/case-studies/morning-brew); [GrowSurf](https://growsurf.com/blog/how-morning-brew-grew-its-subscribers/)). Not Boring had 473 subscribers in March 2020, jumped from 1,885 to 3,850 in two days on a Product Hunt launch, and chose sponsorship over a paywall because the numbers were better ([Curator of Insights](https://curatorofinsights.substack.com/p/not-boring-how-packy-mccormick-built); [Aakash Gupta](https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/packy-mccormicks-story-0-to-80k-subscribers)). Lenny Rachitsky found that **78% of new subscribers came from other newsletters recommending him**, crossed 1 million in March 2025, converts roughly 4–5% of free readers to paid, and summarises his advice as "just stop tweeting and do the work" ([Growth Curve](https://www.growthcurvenewsletter.com/p/how-lenny-rachitsky-built-the-world-s-largest-product-newsletter-with-no-full-time-employees); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/make-it/2024/01/15/lenny-rachitskys-advice-for-success-in-a-podcast-and-newsletter.html)). Platform ad-share, by contrast, is not a business: Nas Daily's 606 million views in one month earned about **$33,000** across all platforms ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/most-youtubers-are-secretly-broke-nas-daily-slams-instagram-facebook-youtube-tiktok-over-revenue-sharing-376233-2023-04-05)). Every surviving brand monetises off-platform: HONY via a $7 Patreon with "not a single sponsored post" ([Patreon](https://www.patreon.com/humansofnewyork)), Letters of Note via books that sold "hundreds of thousands of copies" and roughly 100 Letters Live shows ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/authors/interviews/article/67365-four-questions-for-shaun-usher.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letters_Live)).

The channel data for 2025–26 sets hard constraints on how a new page should distribute. Average Facebook organic engagement is about **0.15% of followers, link posts the worst format at 0.05%**, and more than 95% of feed posts carry no external link ([Socialinsider](https://socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/facebook); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/heres-where-each-of-the-big-social-platform-stands-on-external-links/733946/)). Facebook referrals to news sites fell 50% in the year to May 2024 ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/media-audience-and-business-data/media_metrics/facebooks-referral-traffic-for-publishers-down-50-in-12-months/)); Google Search referrals to publishers fell **33% in 2025**, with small publishers losing about 60% over two years ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/media-audience-and-business-data/google-traffic-down-2025-trends-report-2026/); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/small-publisher-search-traffic-drops-data-471974)). Instagram's ranking signals are watch time, likes per reach and "sends per reach", with sends mattering more for non-followers ([Kompozy](https://kompozy.io/news/instagram-mosseri-ranking-signals-guidance)), which is why a touching story told natively in a carousel outperforms a link to the same story. Facebook is also the hardest platform to grow from zero: only 3.86% of Facebook accounts moved up a follower tier between 2025 and 2026 versus 16.47% on TikTok ([CUNY Pressbooks](https://pressbooks.cuny.edu/inspire/part/instant-fame-on-tiktok-in-2026-innovative-growth-engine-strategies/)). What a new page should take from all this is the following set of moves.

| Move | Evidence it works | Football translation |
|---|---|---|
| One rigid unit, repeated for years | HONY portrait+quote; Nas 1-minute video; Morning Founder 1,200–1,800-word plate | A numbered "Plate"/"Programme" per player or match, same length, same slot |
| Subject-first, creator invisible | Stanton: "the more I removed myself…the faster it has grown" | Open with the subject's own line or the one decision, never editorial throat-clearing |
| Build a backlog before cadence | Morning Founder's 260 researched stories; TWS prototyped for years | Six finished stories and a 90-day on-this-day calendar before launch |
| Serialise | HONY Tanqueray; OGS "Can Ask Meh?"; Lenny mini-series | "Malaysia Cup years" or "The 1994 team" as multi-part series |
| One platform, then owned list | Twitter→Substack (Bloom, Lenny); Facebook→Patreon (HONY) | Instagram carousels or Facebook-native text as reach; email as the product |
| Native posts, not links | FB links 0.05%; Instagram rewards sends | Full story natively, link in first comment or bio |
| Monetise off-platform, labelled | Morning Founder slots; TWS labels every sponsored post; Rice's branded-only model failed | Small labelled sponsorship, Patreon tier, book or zine later; "we say no a lot" |

## Football storytelling is crowded globally but empty in Southeast Asia

The global English-language market splits into four tiers, and none of them is a template a Singapore solo creator can copy directly. At the top, The Athletic (owned by The New York Times) has been profitable for three consecutive quarters as of May 2025 and grew its newsletter list from 3 million to **5 million in a year, with 6 million imminent** ([Axios](https://axios.com/2025/05/20/nyt-athletic-profitable); [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2025/05/06/the-athletic-just-hit-5m-newsletter-subscribers-here-s-how-and-why-it-matters)). Footballco claims a 650 million monthly audience but booked a reported £9.1M loss for 2025 ([MLS](https://www.mlssoccer.com/news/major-league-soccer-expands-partnership-with-footballco-as-a-content-partner-for-the-2025-season); [ResultSense](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-09-07-footballco-ai-world-cup-traffic/)). Men in Blazers raised a **$15M Series A in January 2025** ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/men-in-blazers-media-network-15-million-series-a-raised-for-expanding-soccer-coverage)). The independent long-form tier has converged on about 10,000 print copies and sub-100k social followings: Mundial publishes a quarterly with roughly 10,000 circulation and a core team of five, having needed Footballco to "save" it in 2022 ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/?p=240244); [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/footballco-saves-mundial-and-gets-print)); These Football Times publishes two free long-form articles a day, states in its media pack that the site "does not make money from the website itself", and has a **newsletter list of about 73k and roughly 75k X, 37k Instagram and 25k Facebook followers** ([TFT 2024 media pack](https://thesefootballtimes.co/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/MediaTFTPack.pdf); [TFT 2025 media kit](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0253/7580/1399/files/Media_Kit_8d0d62fc-4341-4133-90f1-ccac09e2b7e3.pdf)). The audience scale in storytelling lives on YouTube: Tifo has about **1.82M subscribers and is still adding ~20k a month** ([Social Blade](https://socialblade.com/youtube/handle/tifo); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@tifo/)), Memorias del Fútbol 1.77M ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/memoriasfutbol)), Football Tales (India) 1.04M ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCHwwMXkgkulM0i1PC_SQIVw)) and HITC Sevens around 685k ([Social Blade](https://socialblade.com/youtube/handle/hitcsevens/videos)). The one-person Substack tier is small: Jonathan Wilson, the most decorated football historian writing in English, launched in January 2025 and sits at roughly 6k subscribers even after growing 40% during the 2026 World Cup ([Substack](https://jonawils.substack.com/about); [Substack interview](https://on.substack.com/p/jonathan-wilsons-world-cup-diary)); Grace Robertson has 20k+ ([Substack](https://substack.com/@graceonfootball)).

| Tier | Example | Size (date) | Model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corporate bundle | The Athletic / Tifo | 6M newsletter (May 2025); 1.82M YouTube (Oct 2026) | Subscriptions + ads |
| Independent magazine | Mundial; These Football Times; The Blizzard; Nutmeg | ~10k print; ~73k list, <100k social | Print, merch, brand studio |
| YouTube history | Memorias del Fútbol; Football Tales; HITC Sevens; Ahmed's Goal | 1.77M; 1.04M; ~685k; ~398k | Ad share, sponsors |
| Solo Substack | Wilson's World; Grace on Football; Trevor Knell | ~6k; 20k+; ~50 paid then quit | Paid tier (hundreds of payers typical) |
| Club-specific paid | The Anfield Wrap | >10,000 payers at £5/month | Subscription + ads |
| Singapore/SEA | The Final Whistle podcast | ~500 plays/episode | Volunteer |

The monetisation lesson from this landscape is blunt: long-form storytelling is the first thing cut inside traffic-funded brands and survives only where it is the paid product or sells something else. Bleacher Report closed B/R Mag because long-form "was not resonating as much with B/R readers" ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/bleacher-report-closing-b-r-mag-long-form-storytelling-ben-osborne-howard-beck/)); a 250-site football blog network changed hands for a "six figure" sum in January 2026 ([SBC News](https://sbcnews.co.uk/marketing/2026/01/26/dave-sport-snack-media/)). The models that work are The Anfield Wrap's **more than 10,000 payers at £5 a month** ([Nieman Lab](https://niemanlab.org/?p=168408)), Classic Football Shirts turning a 6,700-shirt archive into pure storytelling marketing for a £31.8M retail business ([City AM](https://www.cityam.com/?p=2342082); [cllct](https://www.cllct.com/sports-collectibles/memorabilia/soccer-jersey-retailer-classic-football-shirts-draws-38m-investment-from-chernin)), and Mundial's half-and-half split between website/newsletter/merch and brand collaborations ([Press Gazette 2019](https://pressgazette.co.uk/news/mundial-football-magazine-says-business-will-not-live-or-die-by-ad-sales-as-merchandise-and-brand-partnerships-help-profits-grow/)). Note that the exact concept you describe already exists as "The Pyramid", a Substack of "original football stories from around the world" since January 2021 ([The Pyramid](https://thepyramid.substack.com/p/coming-soon)), and that solo faceless football-documentary YouTube is an established, "highly replicable" format whose leading channel nevertheless decayed to one upload every five weeks ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/ahmedsgoal)).

Southeast Asia is the white space. The nearest Singapore substitute is The Final Whistle, a weekly fan podcast by three volunteers that captures "stories of players and coaches, past and present", averages about **500 plays an episode** and has topped Spotify's local sports chart ([Wonderwall.sg](https://wonderwall.sg/people/this-local-football-podcast-is-reigniting-fan-interest-in-the-sp)). Beyond that, storytelling comes from the clubs themselves (Lion City Sailors' "From the Bridge" series ([LCS](https://www.lioncitysailorsfc.sg/from-the-stands-a-love-borne-out-of-ambition-accomplishment-and-a-real-affinity-for-the-singapore-football-fan))), the federation (a March 2026 FAS brand film following Gavin Lee from boyhood to the Lions bench ([Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/money/mediaoutreach/2026/03/23/roar-together-rise-together-football-association-of-singapore-launches-brand-film-celebrating-the-nations-love-for-the-game/455841))), occasional ESPN features linking the Sailors' 2026 continental run to the 1994 Malaysia Cup ([ESPN](https://global.espn.com/football/story/_/id/45174547/reliving-lion-city-sailors-history-making-charge-afc-champions-league-two-final)), and heritage material scattered across non-football outlets such as Remember Singapore's "Generations of Local Football Heroes" ([Remember Singapore](https://remembersingapore.org/2011/06/14/generations-of-local-football-heroes/)). Singapore football history to date is book-shaped and archive-shaped: "ROAR: Football Legends of Singapore", "50 Greatest Moments in Singapore Football", Quah Kim Song's memoir, Dr Nick Aplin's National Archives talks, and the 2007 documentary "The Kallang Wave" ([ActiveSG Circle](https://www.activesgcircle.gov.sg/read/roar-football-legends-of-singapore-for-future-generations-to-remember); [Istana](https://www.istana.gov.sg/newsroom/opening-tony-tan-keng-yam-at-the-book-launch-of-the-50-greatest-moments-in-singapore-football/); [Sinema.sg](https://sinema.sg/2007/04/14/review-the-kallang-wave)). Nobody is doing this continuously online, in English, in the Asian timezone.

The demand picture explains why the gap exists and how to bridge it. Only **35% of Singaporeans supported local football in 2023, but 70% said they could be drawn to it**, with Gen Z (80%) and Millennials (72%) showing the most latent interest, and 40% believe football is influential to Singapore's culture and identity ([The Independent SG](https://theindependent.sg/40-believe-football-is-influential-to-singapores-culture-and-identity-while-35-say-they-support-local-football/)). The actual audience is Premier League-shaped: a cumulative **386 million** Premier League audience across Southeast Asia in 2021/22 and 56% of SEA football supporters are Premier League fans ([Sportfive](https://sportfive.com/beyond-the-match/insights/the-premier-league-in-south-east-asia)); the league is piloting its own Premier League+ streaming service in Singapore from 2026/27 at S$238–380 a year ([Alvinology](https://alvinology.com/2026/08/14/premier-league-launches-in-singapore-giving-fans-more-ways-to-follow-every-match/)). Indonesia's Liga 1 has about 4.5 million Instagram followers against Malaysia's 266k ([SEAsia Stats](https://seasiastats.com/statistic/number-of-football-league-followers-in-southeast-asia-january-2025-fHxlQ)), and Astro reports 575 million digital views across three Liga Malaysia seasons ([Astro](https://corporate.astro.com.my/mediaroom-releases/astro-continues-its-partnership-liga-malaysia-driving-local-football-forward)). The implication is a hybrid: global and Premier League legends as the top of the funnel, Singapore, Malaysia Cup and Southeast Asian stories as the loyal core, and Bahasa Malaysia or Indonesia variants as a later expansion.

## Frank verdict: a passion project with a 36-month horizon, not a business

Three independent lines of evidence point to the same answer. First, the one solo text-first football comparable with disclosed numbers did not reach viability: Trevor Knell had **over 50 paying subscribers after roughly two years and 700-plus articles**, and left Substack at the end of 2025 saying the subscription model "felt more like a struggle than achieving success" ([Football Writing Blog](https://footballwritingblog.substack.com/p/thank-you-for-your-support); [Farewell Substack](https://footballwritingblog.substack.com/p/farewell-substack)). At typical pricing that is a few hundred US dollars a month before fees. Substack's own skew confirms it: "most publications with fewer than 500 subscribers earn little to nothing" ([Best Writing](https://bestwriting.com/substack-statistics)). Second, the discovery channels a text site relies on have deteriorated simultaneously: search down a third, Facebook link reach collapsed, Threads delivering under 0.1% of publisher traffic ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-says-link-posts-ranked-properly-threads-reach/750126/)), and AI chatbot referrals under 1% of pageviews ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/media-audience-and-business-data/google-traffic-down-2025-trends-report-2026/)). Third, attrition is the real killer: **52% of creators report burnout and 37% are actively considering leaving** ([Agility PR](https://www.agilitypr.com/pr-agency-news/burnout-emerges-as-a-barrier-to-growth-in-the-creator-economy-with-half-of-creators-suffering/)), and the niche's own leading solo channel shows "extreme upload inconsistency" ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/ahmedsgoal)).

Against that, four things are genuinely in your favour. Narrative "why did this happen" content is less exposed to AI Overviews than fact-lookup content (dates, scores), and smaller creator sites may be gaining Discover share while large properties lose it, though that finding is hedged and comes from an ad network ([Raptive brief](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2026-03-10-raptive_researchbrief_googlediscovergaming_wp.pdf)). The Southeast Asian wedge is unoccupied, and the primary sources exist: NewspaperSG holds Straits Times coverage from 1845 and more than 220,000 digitised issues, remotely accessible for SPH titles up to roughly 1989/1990 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NewspaperSG); [NLB FAQ](https://eresources.nlb.gov.sg/newspapers/faq)), which covers the entire Malaysia Cup golden era. Living ex-players from the 1970s–1990s are interviewable now and will not be forever; first-person interviews are the one input neither AI nor global channels can replicate. And the Morning Founder formula shows a Singapore solo operator can sustain a daily researched-history unit and sell sponsorship against it.

So the direction is good only under conditions. Treat it as a long-horizon project with modest side income, budget **24–36 months to the first meaningful money** (a small labelled sponsorship, a Patreon tier, a zine or an event), and set a **12-month kill-or-continue checkpoint** judged on email list size, read-completion and newsletter click rate rather than revenue. Differentiate on access, not prose: writing "Maradona at Napoli" from Singapore adds nothing a reader cannot get from fifty channels. Make the website the archive and conversion point, not the front door; the front door is native social (carousels, Facebook-native text, possibly short video) feeding an email list. Use a sustainable cadence, one deep story a fortnight atomised into daily short posts, rather than the daily writing that broke Knell. Apply legal hygiene from day one, because Singapore's defamation environment is unforgiving: a blogger was ordered to pay S$150,000 in 2015 and another S$99,000-plus in 2021 ([Malaysiakini](https://m8.malaysiakini.com/en/world/589751); [Al Jazeera mirror](https://aljazeeranews-3fuh52rgrl.edgeone.app/news/2021/3/25/blogger-ordered-to-pay-singapore-pm-99000-in-defamation-case)). And plan around the calendar you actually have: a late-2026 launch misses the 2026 World Cup bump, so the next hooks are the Asian Cup in January 2027, Singapore national-team and Malaysia Cup anniversaries, and respectfully pre-written profiles of ageing legends.

## The blueprint: content, site, stack, distribution, legal, budget, launch

### Content strategy: a numbered plate, three core pillars, a fortnightly cadence

Define the unit first and never vary it. The recommended unit is Morning Founder-shaped: one footballer, club or match; one decision or turning point that "looked wrong at the time"; 1,200–2,000 words; a number-led one-line hook; a chronological narrative that flags disputed facts in the text; a "Sources" footer; and one consistent illustration (a commissioned or self-made drawing, a simplified kit silhouette, a map or a timeline) rather than a licensed photo. Give the series a collectible name and number (the research notes suggest "Plate" or, for football, "Programme No. 001"), because the numbering signals a canon worth collecting and sharing. Alongside it run two cheap formats: a 300–500-word "On this day" short, driven by a 365-row calendar built once, and a weekly short profile. The Morning Founder's 10-to-20-page brief and two-sources-per-claim rule are the standard to adopt and to state publicly ([About](https://themorningfounder.com/about/)).

Launch with three core pillars and two format pillars, and treat everything else as tags until volume justifies promotion. The pillar table below rates sourcing difficulty (S) and evergreen value (E) on 1–5; these ratings are judgment, not measured data.

| Pillar | S | E | Why and where to source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Footballer profiles, "the player you forgot" | 2 | 5 | Books, newspapers for colour; Wikipedia/RSSSF/11v11 as indexes only |
| Club histories and origin stories | 2 | 5 | Official histories, local newspapers, Wikipedia bibliographies |
| Singapore and Southeast Asia (the wedge) | 2 pre-1990, 3 after | 5 locally | Malaysia Cup from 1921, Choo Seng Quee, the Quah family, Fandi Ahmad's 1980 winner and 1994 title, the Kallang Roar, S.League founding; NewspaperSG remote to ~1990, NLB on-site after |
| Forgotten finals / "the match that changed everything" (format) | 2 | 5 | Match reports, RSSSF line-ups; strong on-this-day hook |
| Cult heroes and one-club men (format) | 2 | 4–5 | Fanzines, forums; verify anecdotes |
| Stadium histories and lost grounds (tag) | 2 | 5 | Good free imagery on Commons; low personality-rights risk |
| Kit histories (tag) | 3 | 4 | Crest/trademark friction; draw simplified kits |
| Women's football pioneers (tag) | 3 | 5 | Sparse archives, under-covered, good Singapore angle |
| Managers, goalkeepers, derby origins, football and migration/politics (tags) | 2–3 | 4–5 | Books; extra care with living figures |
| Where are they now; backroom staff (use sparingly) | 4 | 3–4 | Interview-dependent; privacy and defamation risk |

The Singapore pillar has anchors already visible in the sources: Fandi Ahmad made his Malaysia Cup debut in 1979 and scored the winner as an 18-year-old in the 1980 final against Selangor, Singapore's 23rd title, then led Singapore to its last Malaysia Cup in 1994 ([NLB Infopedia](https://eresources.nlb.gov.sg/infopedia/articles/SIP_1775_2011-07-19.html); [Remember Singapore](https://remembersingapore.org/2011/06/14/generations-of-local-football-heroes/)); the "Kallang Roar" was coined by soccer writer Jeff Low and is still invoked today ([The Independent SG](https://theindependent.sg/malaysia-cup-fandi-uncle-choo-quah-family-n-ganesan-thanks-for-the-warm-and-lasting-memories); [ActiveSG Circle](https://www.activesgcircle.gov.sg/read/15000-strong-kallang-roar-rallies-singapore-lions-to-2-1-win-over-malaysia-in-friendly)). Cross-border nostalgia is live on the Malaysian side too, with a 2026 book on the 2018 Malaysia Cup final and the Sultan of Selangor's Cup framed as a "celebration of rivalry and friendship" ([Bernama](https://bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2569492); [NST](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/sports/football/2024/08/1094909/sultan-selangors-cup-celebration-rivalry-and-friendship-between-two)).

The research workflow is: pick the subject from the on-this-day calendar or pillar backlog; build a skeleton timeline from Wikipedia and RSSSF/11v11 used strictly as indexes (Transfermarkt is user-generated with editorial oversight and its values are biased estimates ([MDSOAR](https://mdsoar.org/items/08e5d64e-ee10-4be4-a8de-1d3982a062c7/full))); confirm every date, score and quote against a contemporary primary source and record it in the story's sources field; write; run a fact-check pass on names, dates, spellings and living/deceased status; source and credit images; publish; derive social outputs. Use the AI assistant for outlining, archive search terms, tightening prose, alt text and social variants, never for facts or quotations without a cited source, and say so on the About page. Realistic cadence is one long-form story a fortnight, one short format a week, one newsletter a fortnight and three or four social posts a week drawn from the archive, roughly six to eight hours a week. Write a one-page style guide: British English, naming conventions for Malay and Chinese names, score and date formats, a dek under 160 characters that doubles as the meta description, an image-credit format, and sensitivity rules for deaths, allegations and private lives.

### Site structure: a thin, fast archive with search, series and sources

Mirror the Morning Founder's economy of pages and add the archive features a storytelling site needs. The content model, defined once as front matter and exposed as form fields in the CMS, should hold title, dek, slug, date, updated, hero image with credit and alt text, pillar, clubs, players, countries, eras (decade buckets), competitions, tags, an optional series name and order, a sources list rendered at the foot of every story, a computed reading time and a draft flag. Page types are: a home page with one featured lead, a "Story of the day" slot driven by the on-this-day calendar, pillar entry points and the newsletter box; a story page with hero and credit, dek, date and reading time, pull-quote components, captioned images, the sources footer, plain-URL share buttons (X, Facebook, WhatsApp, Threads, copy link; no third-party SDKs), related stories by club/pillar/era computed at build, and previous/next within a series; auto-generated index pages for every club, player, country, era, competition, tag and pillar; series landing pages ("Malaysia Cup years"); a reusable timeline component for club histories and careers; an About page carrying the editorial standards, AI-use disclosure and corrections log; an archive by year; a search page; a `/links` page that replaces Linktree; and RSS, sitemap, robots and 404. Plumbing is Open Graph and Twitter-card meta on every page, a per-story share image generated at build, Article JSON-LD with headline, image, datePublished, dateModified and author (Google says this can improve title, image and date display for sports article pages ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/data-types/article))), canonical URLs and a full-text RSS feed. Default to self-hosted or system fonts, responsive AVIF/WebP images with dimensions set, lazy loading, dark mode, mandatory alt text in the CMS schema, and a Lighthouse mobile target of 95-plus on story pages. For search, Pagefind indexes the built site and ships a chunked index so even a 10,000-page site loads under about 300 KB including the library ([CloudCannon](https://cloudcannon.com/blog/introducing-pagefind/)).

### Tech stack: Astro, GitHub Actions, Pages CMS, in a new repository

Start with the repository. Your current `inzyspencersg.github.io` repo holds a single `index.html` dashboard marked `noindex,nofollow`, and its git history is a stream of automated "Dashboard refresh" commits several times a day; dropping the football site into it would trigger constant rebuilds and mix two unrelated projects. Either move the dashboard to its own repo and give the football site the root, or (simpler) create a fresh project repository served at a custom domain. GitHub Pages is free for public repositories, with a 1 GB site size, a 100 GB/month bandwidth soft limit and a 10-builds-per-hour soft limit that does not apply to GitHub Actions deployments ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/github-pages-limits)); GitHub now recommends Actions for Pages deployment ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/ja/enterprise-server@3.14/pages/setting-up-a-github-pages-site-with-jekyll/about-github-pages-and-jekyll)).

The recommended generator is **Astro** with Markdown/MDX content collections, built by the official `withastro/action` and deployed to Pages; for a custom domain set `site` in `astro.config.mjs` and omit `base`, grant the workflow `pages: write` and `id-token: write`, and commit a lockfile ([Astro docs](https://v4.docs.astro.build/en/guides/deploy/github/)). The 2026 practitioner consensus is "pick Astro, unless you have a specific reason", with Hugo winning raw build speed and Eleventy winning simplicity ([jwatte](https://jwatte.com/blog/blog-ssg-comparison-2026/)). Astro earns its place here through component-level features (timelines, pull quotes), and build-time share images via `astro-og-canvas` ([npm](https://npmjs.com/package/astro-og-canvas)) or Satori plus resvg-js ([DEV](https://dev.to/morellodev/generating-og-images-at-build-time-with-satori-and-astro-4b4b)). Jekyll remains the zero-config native option but is pinned to Jekyll 3.10 with a whitelisted plugin set ([pages.github.com/versions](https://pages.github.com/versions)); choose it only if you want no Actions at all. Next.js is not recommended for a content site.

For editing without touching code, use **Pages CMS**: an open-source, hosted editor for static sites stored in GitHub repos, configured by a `.pages.yml` file, usable entirely online with GitHub login and no server to run ([Pages CMS docs](https://pagescms.org/docs/); [Astro docs](https://docs.astro.build/de/guides/cms/pages-cms/)). This avoids the main pain of Decap or Sveltia on GitHub Pages, which need an OAuth server or a Cloudflare Worker that one practitioner described as "you're on your own, and the documentation is... imperfect" ([i40west](https://github.com/i40west/netlify-cms-cloudflare-pages); [dylanbeattie.net](https://dylanbeattie.net/2025/02/13/sveltiacms-jekyll-and-github-pages.html)). Ask the assistant to pin Astro and action versions and set up Dependabot or Renovate with grouped monthly PRs so updates arrive as reviewable bundles.

For the newsletter, pick a provider with a plain HTML form you can embed and keep the static site canonical. Buttondown is free to 100 subscribers then $9/month; Kit has a free tier with one automation; beehiiv Launch is free to 2,500 subscribers with a referral and recommendation network ([superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/articles/best-newsletter-platforms-indie-hackers)). Avoid MailerLite, whose free tier was cut twice in twelve months ([groupmail](https://blog.groupmail.io/mailerlite-free-plan-limits/)). Substack costs nothing until paid revenue, then takes 10% plus Stripe fees ([ruzuku](https://www.ruzuku.com/learn/articles/substack-pricing)); choose it only if you want Substack, not your site, to be the home. Ghost(Pro) Starter at $15–18/month bundles site and newsletter but removes the free-hosting advantage ([typeflo](https://typeflo.io/blog/ghost-pricing-plans)). For analytics, use a cookieless tool so no consent banner is needed: Umami Cloud's free Hobby tier (reported 100K events/month) or GoatCounter (free for non-commercial), with Plausible at about EUR 9/month as the polished paid option ([freetier.co](https://freetier.co/directory/products/umami); [toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/goatcounter/pricing); [Seline](https://seline.com/blog/plausible-analytics-pricing)). Measure unique readers per story at 7 and 90 days, scroll-depth events at 25/50/75/100%, referrers by story, signups per 1,000 readers and newsletter clicks (clicks, not opens, are the reliable metric after Apple Mail Privacy Protection ([bestwriting.com](https://bestwriting.com/newsletters))), plus Google Search Console.

### Distribution: email is the product, native social is the reach

Copy the Morning Founder's core mechanic: the email carries the complete story at a fixed time, the website publishes a week later, and the archive exists to convert searchers and social visitors into subscribers. Then adapt the social layer to football and to 2026 platform behaviour. Post full stories natively: on Instagram, a 1080x1350 carousel generated at build from the same pipeline as the share image (cover slide with the hook, three to six slides of one fact or quote each, a closing CTA), optimised for sends; on Facebook, the text and illustration posted natively with the link in the first comment, because this is where the Malaysia Cup-era generation and groups such as Ultras Malaya organise ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultras_Malaya)); on Threads and X, a hook line plus three to five facts, expecting little traffic. Add a Telegram channel early, since it is the owned channel Singapore brands actually use. Have the build step emit a `social.md` per story with the X/Threads thread, Facebook caption ending in a question (Chue's LinkedIn habit), Instagram caption and alt text, so posting is copy-and-paste with nothing to break. Schedule with Buffer's free plan (about 3 channels, 10 queued posts each) or Metricool's free tier ([marcandrews](https://marcandrews.com/?p=2565); [Metricool](https://metricool.com/metricool-vs-buffer/)), or natively in Meta Business Suite. The two growth levers with documented effect are manual, one-to-one outreach for the first thousand (the Brew-bassador lesson) and recommendation swaps with adjacent newsletters (Lenny's 78%); candidates are Singapore and Malaysian sport, heritage and history newsletters, and English-language football Substacks. Add a referral programme only once the list is in the low thousands. Short video is the fastest-growing discovery format in football, but it is optional in year one; if you try it, follow the scaled history channels and use stills, maps and animation rather than broadcast clips.

### Legal and image basics: draw, credit, avoid crests, mind the living

Match footage is off-limits: the Premier League has pursued clip uploaders since 2014, and FIFA and the Premier League are among the most active Content ID enforcers ([Channel 4 News](https://www.channel4.com/news/football-fans-posting-goals-online-could-face-prosecution); [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/football-clips-for-edits)). Getty editorial captions for English domestic football state that web and mobile use needs an additional licence from the FAPL ([Getty](https://www.gettyimages.com/detail/news-photo/fulham-player-clint-dempsey-celebrates-after-scoring-the-news-photo/77668332)). The low-risk image policy is: a consistent illustration as the default hero, which also solves the share-image problem; Wikimedia Commons for photos, verifying each file page yourself and attributing author, licence and source link, since "from Wikimedia Commons" alone is insufficient and trademarks and personality rights still apply ([Commons:REUSE](https://c.enwp.org/wiki/Commons:REUSE); [Wikimedia Legal](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Legal/CC_BY-SA_licenses_and_social_media)); your own photography of Jalan Besar, the National Stadium and Bishan; stadiums, trophies, programmes and public-domain newspaper-era scans in preference to close-ups of living players; and a handful of **Alamy individual licences from £17 per asset** for flagship stories, checking the editorial-only flag ([Alamy](https://www.alamy.com/help/licensing-and-pricing)). Getty's embed tool was free for editorial non-commercial use at launch, but current terms were not retrievable and embeds cannot serve as hero or share images ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1383852/getty-makes-35-million-images-embeddable-to-gather-data-monetize-usage.html)). Do not use club crests as design elements, in share images or in a clubs grid; they are registered trademarks defended in Arsenal v Reed and in Manchester United's action against Sega ([CITMA](https://citma.shorthandstories.com/multi-issue-magazine-fashion/with-our-blogs/football-clubs-and-trade-marks-badge-of-origin-or-mark-of-allegiance/index.html); [World IP Review](https://www.worldipreview.com/news/judge-rejects-man-utd-s-football-manager-fan-site-claims-19746)); name the club and use colour swatches or kit silhouettes. NewspaperSG republication of SPH articles must be cleared with SPH Media and downloads are watermarked ([NLB FAQ](https://eresources.nlb.gov.sg/newspapers/faq)), so quote and cite rather than reproduce scans.

Quoting is comfortable under Singapore's Copyright Act 2021, which replaced fair dealing with an open-ended, US-style four-factor fair use test ([Law Gazette](https://lawgazette.com.sg/?p=10621)); keep quotations short, attributed and in a sources footer. Defamation is the serious risk. In Singapore, sharing or republishing someone else's defamatory statement is itself publication, even in quotation marks or with a disclaimer ([Thuraisingam](https://thuraisingam.com/news-mentions/defamation-laws-in-singapore-explained/amp)); in the UK, relevant for British clubs and readers, the Defamation Act 2013 requires serious harm and offers truth and honest-opinion defences ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2013/26/enacted)). Adopt a living-persons rule: any allegation about a living person (match-fixing, doping, misconduct) needs a reputable primary source reporting an established fact (conviction, official finding, the person's own admission), phrased as attributed reported fact, and omitted if the story does not need it; prefer deceased subjects and documented events. For the newsletter list, the PDPA requires stating the purpose, obtaining consent, providing an unsubscribe route and ceasing use on withdrawal, and the Spam Control Act requires honouring unsubscribes within 10 business days ([SingaporeLegalAdvice](https://singaporelegaladvice.com/law-articles/email-newsletters-comply-singapore-law); [SSO](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/SCA2007)); a one-line consent note under the form and a short privacy page cover this.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. "Football stories" is not one direction but two: a generic English-language storytelling site, which the evidence says is saturated at every tier and earns nothing on its own, and a Southeast Asian football memory project with global legends as bait, which no one is doing continuously and for which the primary sources (a newspaper archive back to 1845, living ex-players, a Malaysia Cup era that 40% of Singaporeans still consider part of national identity) are unusually accessible. The Morning Founder's real lesson is not that storytelling goes viral but that a solo operator with a tight unit, a deep backlog, a rights-free visual identity and an email-first funnel can sustain a researched-history publication in Singapore and sell labelled sponsorship against it, even without a public audience number.

The implication for how you spend the next 90 days is that the website is the least important part. The build is cheap and solved; what will determine the outcome is whether you have six finished stories and a 365-day calendar before launch, whether the first thousand readers are earned by hand, whether the stories contain things only you could have found, and whether you have decided in advance what "working" means at month twelve. Treat the archive you create as the asset: Classic Football Shirts turned 6,700 shirts into a storytelling moat, and a numbered, sourced, illustrated canon of Southeast Asian football stories is the kind of thing that eventually becomes a book, an exhibition or a partner for a club or federation that has already shown it wants this content made.
