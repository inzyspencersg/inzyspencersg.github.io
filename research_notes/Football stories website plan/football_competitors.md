# Competitive landscape of football (soccer) storytelling media

Research notes compiled 18 October 2026. Method: ~45 web searches; direct page fetches (WebFetch and curl) were blocked by the sandbox proxy for most outlet domains (thesefootballtimes.co, pressgazette.co.uk, digiday.com, socialblade.com, niemanlab.org), so figures below come from search-result snippets of those pages and should be re-verified before publication. Everything is dated where the source allowed; where a date could not be established it is flagged. Many of the smaller accounts named in the brief (e.g. "Football Stories" IG, "Football's Greatest", "The Football Archaeologist", "Tales from the Terraces", "Football Memories") returned no reliable public data and are listed in Gaps.

## Key Question 1: Who are the 15-25 most relevant competitors and roughly how big are they (with dates)?

### Takeaway
The market splits into four tiers: (1) venture/corporate-scale platforms where storytelling is one vertical among many (The Athletic/Tifo, Footballco: Goal/Mundial/Copa90-style, Men in Blazers); (2) independent long-form magazines with small print circulations of ~10k and social footprints under 100k (Mundial, These Football Times, The Blizzard, Nutmeg); (3) YouTube/short-video history channels at 0.6M-1.8M subscribers (Tifo, HITC Sevens, Football Tales, Memorias del Fútbol); and (4) one-person Substacks with 6k-20k+ total subscribers (Jonathan Wilson, Grace Robertson). In Singapore/SEA the storytelling niche is essentially empty of dedicated players; the biggest "competitor" is a fan podcast averaging ~500 plays an episode.

## Key Question 2: What story formats work best, and which have grown fastest 2024-2026?

### Takeaway
The fastest measurable growth 2024-26 is in (a) email newsletters attached to a brand (Athletic +67% YoY to 5M, 2025) and (b) short/mid-form video (Tifo still adding ~20k subs/month in 2026; Juventus Women 0→1.6M TikTok in a year). Independent long-form text sites report flat small audiences and openly say the website itself earns nothing (TFT). Short-form video is where "storytelling" is now being formally rewarded (TikTok-sponsored WFS Best Content Creator award), but verified history-specific creators at the 1M+ level are all on YouTube, not TikTok.

### Cited Findings
- The Athletic newsletters: 3M → 5M in a year (+67%) by May 2025, 6M by late May 2025; most growth in the daily "Pulse" — [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2025/05/06/the-athletic-just-hit-5m-newsletter-subscribers-here-s-how-and-why-it-matters); [Axios](https://axios.com/2025/05/20/nyt-athletic-profitable)
- Tifo (animated 5-15 minute explainers) gaining ~20K subs per 30 days in Oct 2026 at 1.82M — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@tifo/)
- Juventus Women TikTok: 1.6M followers / 230M views in ~12 months from March 2024 launch; TikTok Creator Lab's first "long-form" serialized story — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/dream-like-juventus-women-long-form-storytelling-project-from-juventus-creator-lab-on-tiktok/)
- TikTok claims 51M football-related videos published in the year before the 2025 Club World Cup; 6.5M under #SportsOnTikTok (self-reported) — [Capital FM Africa](https://capitalfm.africa/tiktok-redefines-sports-becoming-the-worlds-go-to-sports-arena/)
- FIFA/TikTok named 30 creators to cover the 2026 World Cup; World Football Summit added a TikTok-presented "Best Content Creator" award for "original storytelling" — [SVG](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2025/06/04/tiktok-and-dazn-in-global-campaign-supporting-fifa-club-world-cup-coverage1/); [WFS](https://worldfootballsummit.com/?p=31133)
- TFT 2024 media pack: two long-form articles per day, free; the website "does not make money" — [TFT 2024 pack](https://thesefootballtimes.co/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/MediaTFTPack.pdf)
- Bleacher Report closed B/R Mag (long-form unit) because long-form "was not resonating as much with B/R readers"; ~10 laid off — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/bleacher-report-closing-b-r-mag-long-form-storytelling-ben-osborne-howard-beck/)
- Substack football conversion benchmarks: one football publication reported ~5.5% free→paid vs an 8-10% "normal" Substack rate — [SCOUTED](https://scoutedftbl.com/our-first-milestone-approaches-the/); a Portuguese football writer had 6,000+ free and 250+ paying subs (>2 years old) — [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/from-print-to-digital-lessons-from-two-european-football-writers-on-growing-paid-newsletters/)
- Jonathan Wilson grew Substack 40% during the 2026 World Cup (event-driven spike) — [Substack](https://on.substack.com/p/jonathan-wilsons-world-cup-diary)
- Reuters Institute DNR 2025 (via mirrors): engagement with TV, print and news websites continued to fall as audiences shift to social, video platforms and aggregators; rise of personality-led/influencer news; newsletters/notifications key for deepening relationships with existing users — [DNR 2025 mirror](https://ruralindiaonline.org/en/library/resource/reuters-institute-digital-news-report-2025)
- Singapore's The Final Whistle podcast: long-conversation format, ~500 plays/episode, topped Spotify SG sports chart (2021-23 reporting) — [Wonderwall.sg](https://wonderwall.sg/people/this-local-football-podcast-is-reigniting-fan-interest-in-the-sp)
- Mundial's 2025 tactic to grow print: one theme per issue aimed at readers "who may not care much about football" — [Press Gazette, June 2025](https://pressgazette.co.uk/?p=240244)

### Inferences
- "Long-form as loss-leader, monetise elsewhere" is the dominant pattern: TFT's free site feeds a magazine and merch; Mundial's magazine feeds a brand studio; the Athletic's features feed a bundle.
- Serialized, character-led narrative works on TikTok when a brand (Juventus) owns the footage. Independent history creators without footage rights have scaled on YouTube using stills, maps and animation (Tifo, Memorias del Fútbol, Football Tales) — a template that avoids match-footage rights.
- The 300-word emotional caption format (Morning-Founder style) has no documented large football practitioner in the sources found, which is itself a signal (see Q5).

### Gaps
- No independent, dated data comparing word-count bands (2000-4000 vs 300 words) to engagement in football; the DNR does not break out sports.
- No verified TikTok/Reels football-history creator with 1M+ followers was found; the "Top football TikTok" lists are dominated by clubs, leagues and comedy accounts — [HypeTrace](https://hypetrace.com/top/tiktok/football/).
- Substack's live sports leaderboard was not readable via search; only 2023 (Tomkins) and undated (SCOUTED 67th→47th) rank points found — [Blogging Guide](https://bloggingguide.com/?p=242798).

## Key Question 3: How do they monetise?

### Takeaway
Only two models are shown to be clearly profitable: scale subscriptions inside a bundle (The Athletic, profitable since Q3 2024) and commerce/brand-partnership hybrids where editorial is the marketing (Mundial, Classic Football Shirts). Pure long-form websites explicitly say they earn nothing; independents survive on print subscriptions (~10k), merch, brand studios, Patreon-style paid newsletters (hundreds to low-thousands of payers), and sponsorship/VC in the podcast tier.

### Cited Findings
- The Athletic: subscriptions + ads (BetMGM, eBay, StubHub); Q1 2025 adjusted operating profit $2.9M vs $8.7M loss — [Axios](https://axios.com/2025/05/20/nyt-athletic-profitable); [A Media Operator](https://www.amediaoperator.com/news/the-athletic-the-ny-timess-crowning-achievement-in-1st-quarter/)
- Mundial: print sales, e-commerce (since 2015), brand partnerships via creative studio; ad sales explicitly not the business — [Press Gazette 2019](https://pressgazette.co.uk/news/mundial-football-magazine-says-business-will-not-live-or-die-by-ad-sales-as-merchandise-and-brand-partnerships-help-profits-grow/)
- TFT: free site makes no money; revenue from bi-monthly magazine (44 countries), merch (Shopify store hosts its media kit), partnerships/sponsored content pitched via media kit — [TFT 2024 pack](https://thesefootballtimes.co/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/MediaTFTPack.pdf); [TFT 2025 kit](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0253/7580/1399/files/Media_Kit_8d0d62fc-4341-4133-90f1-ccac09e2b7e3.pdf)
- The Blizzard: digital $20.99/yr or $5.99-6.99 per issue via Magzter/App Store — [Magzter](https://react.magzter.com/GB/Platform-Media/The-Blizzard/Sports); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6504342651)
- Nutmeg: quarterly print subscriptions, gift bumper packs, Substack, podcast, events — [Nutmeg](https://www.nutmegmagazine.co.uk/product/christmas-gift-bumper-pack-2025-2)
- Anfield Wrap: >10,000 payers at £5/month (≈£600k/yr implied) plus Audioboom ads — [Nieman Lab](https://niemanlab.org/?p=168408)
- Men in Blazers: sponsorship, TV deals, merch, plus $15M VC (Jan 2025) — [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/men-in-blazers-media-network-15-million-series-a-raised-for-expanding-soccer-coverage); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_Blazers)
- COPA90: brand-funded content, broadcaster/streamer sales via Copa90 Studios, investor capital (Liberty Global 14% for £7M in 2015; Turner 2017; Infront 2018) — [SportBusiness](https://media.sportbusiness.com/news/liberty-global-invests-in-copa90/); [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/football-has-lost-its-soul-how-copa90-is-repositioning-itself-around-the-creator-economy/)
- Footballco/Goal: display + branded content (branded growing faster) + affiliate (AI generates 10x affiliate content), yet £9.1M loss 2025 — [ResultSense](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-09-07-footballco-ai-world-cup-traffic/)
- Classic Football Shirts: retail turnover £31.8M; storytelling archive is a marketing asset, never sold — [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/?p=2342082); [cllct](https://www.cllct.com/sports-collectibles/memorabilia/soccer-jersey-retailer-classic-football-shirts-draws-38m-investment-from-chernin)
- Substack independents: Wilson (paid tier from March 2025, 6k+ total); Grace on Football 20k+ total, paid undisclosed; typical football payers in the hundreds — [Wilson about](https://jonawils.substack.com/about); [Grace](https://substack.com/@graceonfootball); [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/from-print-to-digital-lessons-from-two-european-football-writers-on-growing-paid-newsletters/)
- YouTube ad income (HITC Sevens): third-party estimate only — [Networthspot](https://networthspot.com/hitc-sevens/net-worth/)

### Inferences
- A new entrant should not plan on website ads or paid articles; realistic early revenue is (1) a small paid newsletter tier (expect 5-10% conversion), (2) physical product (print/zine/merch), (3) brand or club partnership content, in that order of ease.

- No merch revenue split for TFT or Nutmeg.

## Key Question 4: Which have failed or shut down, and why?

### Takeaway
Closures cluster around three causes: rights/broadcast strategy shifts (FOX Sports Asia, Eleven Asia, B/R Football), long-form "not resonating" inside traffic-driven brands (B/R Mag, Vice Sports), and founder-dependent volunteer sites running out of energy (The Set Pieces dormancy). Corporate rescue (Footballco buying Mundial) and VC reinvention (COPA90 creator pivot) are the two survival paths observed.

### Cited Findings
- **FOX Sports Asia**: Disney announced April 2021 the closure of the pan-Asian sports pay-TV operation (Fox Sports 1-3, Star Sports 1-2) as part of a shift to streaming; shut 1 Oct 2021; SPOTV (Eclat Media) launched to fill the gap — [Inside World Football, 30 Apr 2021](https://www.insideworldfootball.com/2021/04/30/pan-asian-broadcast-shake-fox-sports-announces-closure/); [Sportcal](https://www.sportcal.com/media/disney-closing-fox-sports-and-star-sports-channels-in-southeast-asia-in-d2c-shift/); [SportBusiness](https://media.sportbusiness.com/organisation/fox-sports-asia/)

### Inferences
- Long-form storytelling has repeatedly been the first thing cut inside ad/traffic-funded brands; it survives where it is the product people pay for (Blizzard, Anfield Wrap) or where it sells something else (Mundial, CFS).
- In SEA, the exits were all broadcasters whose economics were rights-driven; none were storytelling brands, so there is no evidence that storytelling per se failed in the region — it was never seriously attempted at scale.

## Key Question 6: Legal/practical constraints (photos, Getty, crests, match footage)

### Takeaway
Match footage is the hardest constraint: the Premier League has pursued clip uploaders since 2007/2014, UK law has no general fair-use defence (only narrow quotation/criticism/news exceptions), and FIFA and the Premier League are among the most active Content ID enforcers. Editorial photo licences (Getty) are robust but tightly scoped, and Premier League imagery carries an additional FAPL licence requirement for web/mobile use. The history channels that have scaled (Tifo, Memorias del Fútbol) do so with animation, maps and licensed stills rather than broadcast clips.

### Cited Findings
- Premier League v YouTube/Google (filed 2007) ended in voluntary dismissal in 2013 with each side paying its own costs; no ruling on fair use, so no precedent — [SportBusiness](https://media.sportbusiness.com/news/premier-league-settles-youtube-copyright-case/); [Calcio e Finanza, Nov 2013](https://www.calcioefinanza.it/2013/11/12/la-premier-league-rinuncia-alla-class-action-contro-youtube-e-google/)
- Aug 2014: PL said posting fan goal clips breaks copyright and it was building "gif crawlers"/"Vine crawlers" with Twitter; lawyers split on whether short clips are a "substantial part" and whether the news-reporting exception applies (Eleonora Rosati) — [Channel 4 News, 15 Aug 2014](https://www.channel4.com/news/football-fans-posting-goals-online-could-face-prosecution); [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/the-premier-leagues-video-clampdown-is-another-struggle-to-monetise-social-media-30591)
- UK case law: no property in a sporting event per se; the copyright is in the broadcast, so footage filmed in-stadium by the uploader is legally distinct from off-broadcast clips — [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/uk/copyright/340360/digital-and-social-media-rights-playing-the-advantage-or-should-we-call-foul)
- Recording and republishing a broadcast, even edited short clips, creates an unauthorised copy; FIFA and the Premier League are among the most active rightsholders in YouTube Content ID enforcement (advocacy guide, not a ruling) — [FluxNote guide](https://fluxnote.io/guides/football-clips-for-edits)
- Getty editorial captions for English domestic football state web/mobile use requires a licence from the FAPL in addition to Getty's licence — [Getty example](https://www.gettyimages.com/detail/news-photo/fulham-player-clint-dempsey-celebrates-after-scoring-the-news-photo/77668332)
- Getty editorial licences are "tightly scoped and prohibit promotional use" but legally robust — [American Football International guide](https://www.americanfootballinternational.com/using-editorial-football-photography-without-infringing-rights/amp/)
- Near-live clip rights are sold to news publishers behind paywalls; unofficial clips dilute that value, which is why leagues enforce — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/the-premier-leagues-video-clampdown-is-another-struggle-to-monetise-social-media-30591)
- Scaled history channels rely on animation/narration rather than clips: Tifo (animated), Memorias del Fútbol ("cinematic, long-form narratives") — [Social Blade](https://socialblade.com/youtube/handle/tifo); [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/memoriasfutbol)
- Classic Football Shirts shows an alternative: own the physical artefacts (6,700+ shirts) and photograph them yourself — [cllct](https://www.cllct.com/sports-collectibles/memorabilia/soccer-jersey-retailer-classic-football-shirts-draws-38m-investment-from-chernin)

### Inferences
- For a Singapore-based publisher the operative law is Singapore's (fair dealing) and the platform's (Content ID), not US fair use; stills, own photography, interviews, illustration and public-domain/archival material (e.g. National Archives) are the low-risk inputs.
- Club crests: no source found; treat as trademarks and use only in editorial context without implying endorsement (inference, not sourced).

### Gaps
- No current (2024-26) Premier League social-clip policy text or club-crest trademark guidance found.
- No Singapore-specific copyright/fair-dealing analysis for sports media found.
- Getty/Alamy/Imago price points for editorial football stills not found.
