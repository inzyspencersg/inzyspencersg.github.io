# Viability of a "Football Stories" Website + Social Page (Solo Creator, Singapore, launch late 2026)

Research date: 18 October 2026. Method note: WebFetch failed (DNS) on every host attempted during this session, so all findings below derive from search-result excerpts of the cited pages rather than full-page reads. Figures are dated where the source gave a date; treat undated third-party aggregator numbers with caution.

## Key Question 1: Audience demand (Singapore, SEA, global; evergreen vs event-driven)

### Takeaway
Football fandom in Singapore and SEA is large and overwhelmingly Premier-League-centric; the regional audience for English-language football content clearly exists. But I found no direct search-volume data for "football history/stories" topics, and no hard figures for Singapore Premier League (SPL) viewership or attendance in 2025-26 — local-football demand is a documented soft spot (35% support in 2023), not a proven audience.

### Cited Findings
- 2023 Yahoo/YouGov survey (July 2023, >2,000 Singapore respondents): only 35% of Singaporeans currently support local football (clubs or national team); 70% said they would be drawn to it if a condition were met; Gen Z (80%) and Millennials (72%) showed most potential interest; 40% believe football is influential to Singapore's culture and identity — [The Independent SG](https://theindependent.sg/40-believe-football-is-influential-to-singapores-culture-and-identity-while-35-say-they-support-local-football/); [Marketech APAC](https://marketech-apac.com/around-40-of-singaporeans-believe-football-is-influential-to-nations-culture-and-identity/)
- Same 2023 study: the three most-watched leagues in Singapore were the English Premier League, UEFA Champions League, and SPL (in that order) — [Marketech APAC](https://marketech-apac.com/?p=77351)
- Older YouGov Asia-Pacific survey (pre-2018): 67% of Singaporean football fans followed the EPL vs 24% the then S-League (regional domestic-league average 39%); EPL was the most-followed league across Asia at 51% — [YouGov](https://business.yougov.com/content/13719-asian-football-fans-en)
- Premier League cumulative SEA audience reached 386 million in 2021/22 (Nielsen, via Sportfive); 56% of football supporters in SEA are Premier League fans (GWI, 2023) — [Sportfive](https://sportfive.com/beyond-the-match/insights/the-premier-league-in-south-east-asia)
- Thailand NIDA Poll (Aug 2026, n=2,500): 55.24% follow the Premier League occasionally, 11.60% regularly; Liverpool most popular (38.54%) — [Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/sport/40069845)
- Indonesia's Liga 1 had ~4.5 million Instagram followers (Jan 2025), the most of any SEA league — [SeasiaStats](https://seasiastats.com/statistic/number-of-football-league-followers-in-southeast-asia-january-2025-fHxlQ)
- ASEAN Mitsubishi Electric Cup 2024: social impressions up 40% to 6 billion (AFF figure, organiser-reported) — [ASEAN Football](https://www.aseanfootball.org/v3/?p=55226)
- Nexxen Singapore World Cup 2026 forecast (May 2026, >1,000 adults): 87% would keep watching after their team exits; OTT/CTV viewing up 27% vs 2022; "the past three tournaments have delivered consistent viewership in Singapore" — [Nexxen](https://nexxen.com/2026-fifa-singapore/); [Branding in Asia](https://www.brandinginasia.com/report-predicts-2026-fifa-world-cup-audiences-in-singapore-will-shift-across-screens-and-dayparts/)
- Premier League is piloting its own "Premier League+" streaming service in Singapore from the 2026/27 season under a six-year StarHub deal (pass pricing S$238/yr for StarHub customers, S$380 for others) — a signal the league views Singapore as a priority test market — [Inside World Football](https://www.insideworldfootball.com/2026/03/02/premier-league-streaming-app-launch-singapore-starhub/); [Alvinology](https://alvinology.com/2026/08/14/premier-league-launches-in-singapore-giving-fans-more-ways-to-follow-every-match/)
- StarHub launched a "Football for All" carnival to close a Premier League season, indicating active local fan-event marketing — [Marketech APAC](https://marketech-apac.com/starhub-to-host-football-for-all-carnival-to-close-premier-league-season)
- 2023 Straits Times "Hard Tackle" podcast: SPL "continues to see dwindling numbers" while European club friendlies drew large crowds — [Razor TV](https://razor.tv/podcast/s1e5-why-european-football-matters-more-to-singaporeans-hard-tackle/4800266888001/5758)
- SPL 2025-26 season ran 23 Aug 2025 to 18 May 2026 with 8 clubs; Lion City Sailors won; Brunei DPMM left before the season — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Singapore_Premier_League)
- Nostalgia/retro demand signal (weak sourcing): "vintage soccer jersey" averages ~14,800 monthly searches worldwide; US "soccercore" searches reportedly up 302% in a year (Trendalytics via filmdaily — could not trace to primary data) — [filmdaily](https://filmdaily.co/news/gen-z-digs-the-real-madrid-jersey-vintage-hits-trend/); Front Office Sports (May 2025) reports pre-owned shirts have become prized by fans, fashion buyers and investors — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/?p=156265)
- Solo "faceless" football-documentary YouTube channels exist in this exact niche: Ahmed's Goal (~398K subs over ~13 years; format = solo mini-documentary of an emotionally charged real event framed as mystery/injustice) and The Football Narrative (top video ~1.3M views, 11.5x its median) — [OutlierKit: Ahmed's Goal](https://outlierkit.com/channel/ahmedsgoal); [OutlierKit: Football Narrative](https://outlierkit.com/channel/footballnarrative24)

### Inferences
- The addressable audience for English-language football *stories* in SG/SEA is really the Premier League/European fan base, not SPL fans. Content about Singapore football history is a differentiator but a small market (35% "support" in 2023, with no evidence of growth since).
- World Cup 2026 (June-July 2026) will have passed by a late-2026 launch; the next big event spike is Asian Cup 2027 (Saudi Arabia, Jan 2027) and the ASEAN Championship cycle. A late-2026 launch misses the 2026 bump and must rely on evergreen pull and anniversaries/deaths, which are unpredictable.
- The retro-shirt/"blokecore" wave suggests cultural appetite for football nostalgia among Gen Z, which aligns with storytelling content, but the quantitative evidence is thin.

### Gaps
- No Google Trends or keyword-volume data found for "football history", "football stories" or specific legend names; the report writer should treat evergreen demand as plausible but unquantified.
- No 2025-26 SPL attendance or TV viewership figures found (searched Wikipedia, spl.sg, news); the only available signal is the 2023 "dwindling" commentary.
- No Premier League fan headcount for Singapore specifically; StarHub does not disclose EPL subscriber numbers.
- No 2025 dedicated study of SEA football social-media engagement; only follower counts and one single-club Thai study.

## Key Question 2: Discoverability 2025-2026 (search, Discover, AI Overviews, social platforms, growing formats)

### Takeaway
Every distribution channel a text-first solo site depends on has deteriorated: Google Search referrals to publishers fell roughly a third in 2025 (small publishers worse, ~-60% over two years), Discover fell 21%, Facebook and X link reach has collapsed, and Threads sends negligible traffic. The formats that are growing are short-form video and owned channels (newsletters/podcasts), not blog posts.

### Cited Findings
- Chartbeat data (Reuters Institute Trends & Predictions 2026, Jan 2026): Google Search referrals to >2,500 publisher sites down 33% year-on-year to Nov 2025; Google Discover down 21%; Facebook up 9%. US-only: Search -38%, Discover -29%. AI chatbot referrals <1% of publisher pageviews — [Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/media-audience-and-business-data/google-traffic-down-2025-trends-report-2026/); [Editor & Publisher](https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/global-publisher-google-traffic-dropped-by-a-third-in-2025,259592)
- Separate Chartbeat analysis: Google Search pageviews fell 34% Dec 2024 to Dec 2025, and small publishers lost about 60% of search referrals over two years — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/small-publisher-search-traffic-drops-data-471974); [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/report-ai-overviews-are-crushing-small-publishers-as-google-search-traffic-drops-60)
- Conflicting dataset: another Chartbeat read (Nov 2024-Nov 2025) put Discover down only 2% while Search fell 33% — the Discover figure varies by window and sample — [TechCentral](https://www.techcentral.ie/ai-search-results-have-publishers-on-the-back-foot-to-maintain-audiences/)
- Google began placing AI summaries in Discover in the US in mid-2025, saying the feature would focus on lifestyle topics "such as sports and entertainment" — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/15/google-discover-adds-ai-summaries-threatening-publishers-with-further-traffic-declines)
- Raptive research brief (Mar 2026, Chartbeat data): Discover was stable/growing in H1 2025, then declined in aggregate in Q3-Q4 2025; the decline was concentrated among larger media properties, "with smaller independent publishers and creator sites potentially gaining share"; answer-type queries (dates, stats) are the ones AI Overviews absorb without a click — [Raptive brief (PDF)](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2026-03-10-raptive_researchbrief_googlediscovergaming_wp.pdf)
- Facebook referrals to news sites down ~80% since Sept 2020 and X down ~60% (Similarweb via Axios); Meta's own data shows views of Facebook link posts fell almost 50% over two years; >95% of posts in Facebook feeds contain no external link — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/new-report-shows-referral-traffic-facebook-x-continued-decline/695550/); [Social Media Today (platform link stances)](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/heres-where-each-of-the-big-social-platform-stands-on-external-links/733946/)
- Threads drove only 51.8M clicks to publishers across May-June (one period), <0.1% of total publisher traffic vs Facebook's 2-3%; Mosseri says links are now "ranked properly" but that does not mean more reach — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-says-link-posts-ranked-properly-threads-reach/750126/); [Stacked Marketer](https://www.stackedmarketer.com/?p=60063)
- EFF left X saying posts now receive <3% of the views a single tweet previously got; NPR found X was <2% of its traffic before leaving — [Mezha](https://mezha.net/eng/?p=1835862); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/npr-says-that-leaving-x-has-had-a-minor-impact-on-referral-traffic/696489/)
- Reuters Digital News Report 2025 Singapore (n=2,014): 45% trust most news most of the time; Straits Times trusted by 75%, CNA 74%; CNA weekly online reach 47%, ST 41% — [Reuters Institute DNR 2025 PDF](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2025-06/Digital_News-Report_2025.pdf); [The Independent SG](https://theindependent.sg/singapores-mainstream-media-more-trusted-than-followed-online/)
- Sportskeeda Football's host said short-form content had "grown the channel substantially"; Zac Djellab reached 100K YouTube subs by focusing on football *entertainment* rather than the crowded news/analysis space — [Creator DB / Sportskeeda](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/reboundfootball/); [Drop Media](https://www.dropmedia.co.uk/?p=7992)
- Google calls mass AI content "scaled content abuse" and announced crackdowns after the Sports Illustrated fake-byline scandal (2023-24); DoubleVerify found >200 AI-content sites ("Synthetic Echo") impersonating ESPN, BBC etc. for programmatic ad revenue — [Futurism](https://futurism.com/google-advon-partnership); [GetCoAI](https://about.getcoai.com/news/ai-powered-websites-are-impersonating-major-sports-news-outlets)

### Inferences
- A text-only website launched in late 2026 cannot expect Google to build its audience; search is a tail-end channel for evergreen pieces, not an engine. The realistic engine is short-form video (TikTok/Reels/Shorts) driving to an owned list (email) — the website becomes the archive and conversion point, not the front door.
- Narrative/"why did this happen" long-form is less exposed to AI Overviews than fact-lookup content (dates, scores, stats), which is a modest structural advantage for storytelling vs news/stats sites.
- Raptive's note that smaller creator sites may be gaining Discover share is the one hopeful data point, but it is hedged ("potentially") and comes from an ad network with an interest in that narrative.

### Gaps
- No sports-specific AI Overviews impact study found.
- No data on how Instagram carousels or TikTok treat text-storytelling specifically; only inference from general platform link policies.
- Could not read the Raptive PDF or Press Gazette article in full (fetch failures); figures are from search excerpts.

## Key Question 3: Monetisation realism

### Takeaway
Direct evidence from a comparable creator (Trevor Knell, football writing Substack) shows ~50 paying subscribers after two years and 700+ articles, and an exit from paid Substack at end-2025 calling it "a struggle". Platform totals are large but earnings are extremely skewed; affiliate rates in the obvious category (retro shirts) are ~5%; no AdSense RPM benchmark for SG sports content was found. Realistic Year-1 income for a solo football-stories site is in the hundreds of dollars per month at best unless video or a paid community takes off.

### Cited Findings
- Trevor Knell's football-writing Substack: >50 paying subscribers and >700 articles by April 2024 (roughly two years after going paid); announced he was leaving Substack at end of 2025, saying the subscription model "felt more like a struggle than achieving success" — [Thank you for your support](https://footballwritingblog.substack.com/p/thank-you-for-your-support); [Farewell Substack](https://footballwritingblog.substack.com/p/farewell-substack)
- Substack platform: >5 million paid subscriptions (Mar 2025); gross writer revenue ~US$450M in 2025 (vs ~$370M 2024); 50+ creators earn >$1M/yr while "most publications with fewer than 500 subscribers earn little to nothing"; high earners migrating to beehiiv/Ghost to avoid the 10% fee — [Best Writing](https://bestwriting.com/substack-statistics); [Sacra](https://www.sacra.com/c/substack); [AOL/Substack 5M](https://www.aol.com/substack-surpasses-5-million-paid-193615026.html)
- Patreon: podcasts earned >US$629M in 2025 (+33% YoY); >47,000 podcasters earning; Patreon takes ~10% — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/04/08/patreon-podcasts-annual-revenue-629-million/); [RightsTech](https://rightstech.com/2026/04/patreon-says-podcasters-earned-629-million-in-2025-platforms-biggest-content-category/)
- Classic Football Shirts affiliate (Awin, US programme): flat 5% commission on new, existing and sale items, 30-day cookie; third-party listings claim 5-10.4% but conflict with the Awin terms — [Awin terms](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile-terms/115317); [FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/classic-football-shirts-affiliate-program/)
- Cult Kits also runs an affiliate programme (Webgains) — [Webgains](https://www.webgains.com/public/en/directory/cult-kits/)
- Substack-adjacent sports storytelling newsletters: The Underdog Newsletter (launched 2017) has >11,000 subscribers; The Gist (general sports, team-run) ~400,000 — [Inbox Billboards](https://inboxbillboards.beehiiv.com/p/championing-underdog-stories-sports); [Nathan Barry](https://nathanbarry.com/062-ellen-hyslop-turn-pain-points-400k-subscriber-newsletter/)
- The Pyramid ("original football stories from around the world") launched on Substack Jan 2021 — the exact concept already exists — [The Pyramid](https://thepyramid.substack.com/p/coming-soon)
- Mundial (long-form football storytelling magazine) could not survive independently: print paused in the pandemic and it was acquired by Footballco in Feb 2022 — [Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/the-wire/footballco-acquires-long-form-magazine-mundial/); [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2022/02/28/footballco-saves-mundial-and-gets-print)
- OutlierKit estimates The Football Narrative channel's value at US$22K-112K (third-party estimate, not verified income) — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/footballnarrative24)
- Podcast research (Creators 2025 report): only 27% of creators who quit had ever monetised vs 56% of active creators — [Radio Ink](https://radioink.com/2025/12/11/podcast-growth-masks-sustainability-gap-new-report-warns/)
- AdSense is detected on ~45.7% of Singapore sites using ad platforms (2026) but no RPM benchmark was found — [WMTips](https://www.wmtips.com/technologies/advertising/country/sg/)
- Premier League+ pass costs S$238-380/yr in Singapore (2026/27) — relevant as a potential affiliate/sponsor category and as a signal of what fans already pay — [Alvinology](https://alvinology.com/2026/08/14/premier-league-launches-in-singapore-giving-fans-more-ways-to-follow-every-match/)

### Inferences
- Benchmarking from Knell: 50 paid subs at a typical US$5-8/month is roughly US$250-400/month before the 10% platform fee and card fees — after two years of near-daily output. That is the documented comparable, not an outlier failure.
- Affiliate at 5% on a S$60-120 retro shirt yields S$3-6 per sale; meaningful affiliate income requires thousands of monthly clicks, which the discoverability data says a new site will not have.
- Display ads are the weakest path: a late-2026 site with low search traffic in a region with modest CPMs will earn trivial AdSense; no SG sports RPM benchmark exists to argue otherwise.
- The monetisation paths with any upside are (a) YouTube/short-form video revenue and sponsorships once a channel is established, (b) a small paid community/Patreon for a loyal niche (e.g. Singapore/Malaysia Cup history), (c) a book or events. All take 2+ years.

### Gaps

- No sponsorship rate cards for small football creators in SEA.
- No earnings data for football-specific Patreon creators.
- Could not confirm Mundial's audience size or revenue.

## Key Question 4: Competition intensity vs differentiation

### Takeaway
Global English-language football storytelling is saturated at every tier (Footballco/Mundial, The Athletic, dozens of faceless YouTube documentary channels, Substacks like The Pyramid). The genuinely under-served seams are Singapore/Malaya Cup-era history and Southeast Asian player stories, where existing output is sporadic books, government-archive talks and a 2007 documentary rather than an ongoing digital publisher.

### Cited Findings
- Faceless football-documentary YouTube is an established, "highly replicable" format: Ahmed's Goal (~398K subs, but upload cadence slipped to ~one video per five weeks), The Football Narrative (OutlierKit profile) — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/ahmedsgoal); [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/footballnarrative24)
- The Pyramid Substack ("original football stories from around the world", since Jan 2021) — direct concept overlap — [The Pyramid](https://thepyramid.substack.com/p/coming-soon)
- Mundial absorbed by Footballco (2022), i.e. the premium storytelling tier is now backed by a large network — [Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/the-wire/footballco-acquires-long-form-magazine-mundial/)
- Zac Djellab's growth came from "a deliberate focus on football entertainment rather than competing in the more established football news and analysis space" — [Drop Media](https://www.dropmedia.co.uk/?p=7992)
- Singapore football history output to date is book- and archive-shaped: "ROAR: Football Legends of Singapore" (fan-authored, launched Jalan Besar Stadium Aug 2022); "50 Greatest Moments in Singapore Football" (volunteer project incl. 1965 and 1977 Malaysia Cup wins); Quah Kim Song memoir on the 1977 final; Dr Nick Aplin's National Archives talk/book on the Malaya Cup; the only documentary found is "The Kallang Wave" (2007) — [ActiveSG Circle](https://www.activesgcircle.gov.sg/read/roar-football-legends-of-singapore-for-future-generations-to-remember); [Istana](https://www.istana.gov.sg/newsroom/opening-tony-tan-keng-yam-at-the-book-launch-of-the-50-greatest-moments-in-singapore-football/); [SMU](https://news.smu.edu.sg/news/2014/11/07/past-success-built-teamwork-quah); [NHB](https://nhb.gov.sg/brasbasahbugis/whats-on/programmes/archives-invites-dr-nick-aplin-sport-in-singapore-and-the-malaya-cup); [Sinema.sg](https://sinema.sg/2007/04/14/review-the-kallang-wave)
- Malaysian side: a 2026 book "KEJOR: 120 Crazy Minutes in Shah Alam" on the 2018 Malaysia Cup final; Sultan of Selangor's Cup framed as "celebration of rivalry and friendship" between Singapore and Selangor — the cross-border nostalgia audience is active — [Bernama](https://bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2569492); [NST](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/sports/football/2024/08/1094909/sultan-selangors-cup-celebration-rivalry-and-friendship-between-two)
- Singapore TikTok "soccer influencers" exist at scale (aggregator lists top profiles at ~3.6M followers, 9% engagement) but these are unnamed entertainment accounts, not storytelling — [Scrumball](https://www.scrumball.com/ranking/top-football-influencers-on-tiktok-in-singapore)
- Vietnam is cited as one of the top-two countries globally for following the Premier League online; Thailand the other (GWI) — [GWI](https://blog.gwi.com/?p=9489)
- Reuters DNR 2025: Singapore mainstream outlets (ST, CNA) are highly trusted but have lower online reach than trust, leaving room for niche independents in topics they under-cover — [The Independent SG](https://theindependent.sg/singapores-mainstream-media-more-trusted-than-followed-online/)

### Inferences
- Writing generic "Maradona at Napoli" pieces from Singapore adds nothing a reader cannot get from 50 existing channels; it is the lowest-differentiation, highest-competition option.
- The defensible wedge is local access: Singapore and Malaysia Cup history, SEA players abroad, Asian-timezone fan culture (3am kick-offs, kopitiam viewing), and interviews with ageing ex-players whose stories are not yet online. These are small audiences but ones no large network is serving continuously.
- A hybrid is plausible: use global-legend stories as discovery bait on short-form video, and local primary-source stories as the paid/loyal core.

### Gaps
- No market-size data for Singapore/Malaysia football-history interest beyond the 2023 35%/40% figures.
- No audit found of existing Singapore football-history blogs or social accounts (searches returned influencer aggregators only).

## Key Question 5: Risks (copyright, defamation, platform dependency, burnout, AI flood)

### Takeaway
All five risks are real and documented. Singapore's defamation environment is unusually unforgiving for individual bloggers; photo rights are contested even among big players; platforms have already throttled links; burnout affects a majority of creators; and AI-generated sports sites are already flooding search. Original interviews and primary sources are the only reliable moat.

### Cited Findings
- Defamation in Singapore: blogger ordered to pay the PM S$150,000 (Roy Ngerng, 2015) and another ordered to pay S$99,000+ in 2021 over a shared article; senior figures have repeatedly sued online commentators — [Al Jazeera mirror](https://aljazeeranews-3fuh52rgrl.edgeone.app/news/2021/3/25/blogger-ordered-to-pay-singapore-pm-99000-in-defamation-case); [Malaysiakini](https://m8.malaysiakini.com/en/world/589751); [HRW 2014](https://www.hrw.org/news/2014/06/24/dispatches-perils-blogging-singapore)
- Academic guidance for Singapore bloggers ("Bloggers Beware: The Five Commandments") from SMU law school exists — [SMU](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/sol_research/448/)
- POFMA (2019) has been used against critics and to block sites (CIVICUS 2023, advocacy source) — [CIVICUS](https://monitor.civicus.org/explore/singapore-authorities-ban-expression-on-israel-palestine-conflict-and-use-pofma-law-to-harass-critics-block-site/)
- Photo rights: sports photographers sued the NFL, Getty and AP (2013) over licensing of their images; a 2016 suit accused Getty of asserting rights over 47K sports photos it did not own — illustrating how contested football-image ownership is — [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2013/10/23/football-photogs-sue-nfl-getty-ap-complex-rights-case/); [Law360](https://law360.com/articles/823970/getty-stole-47k-sports-photos-suit-says)
- Platform dependency: Facebook news referrals -80% since 2020, X -60%, Facebook link-post views -50% in two years; Google Search referrals -33% in 2025 — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/new-report-shows-referral-traffic-facebook-x-continued-decline/695550/); [Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/media-audience-and-business-data/google-traffic-down-2025-trends-report-2026/)
- Burnout: Billion Dollar Boy/Censuswide (July 2025, n=1,000 US/UK creators): 52% have experienced burnout, 37% actively considering leaving — [Agility PR](https://www.agilitypr.com/pr-agency-news/burnout-emerges-as-a-barrier-to-growth-in-the-creator-economy-with-half-of-creators-suffering/); a 2026 survey of 2,400 full-time creators: 62% severe burnout symptoms, nearly half considered quitting in past six months — [SelfEmployed](https://www.selfemployed.com/news/creator-burnout-2026-solo-creators/)
- Cadence failure is visible in the niche: Ahmed's Goal's upload rate decayed to ~one per five weeks ("extreme upload inconsistency") — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/ahmedsgoal)
- Knell: 700+ articles in ~2 years for ~50 paid subs, then exit — [Football Writing Blog](https://footballwritingblog.substack.com/p/farewell-substack)
- AI flood: >200 AI-content sites impersonating ESPN/BBC etc. (DoubleVerify "Synthetic Echo"); Google labels this "scaled content abuse"; Google News "struggling to keep AI-generated swill out" (404 Media via Futurism) — [GetCoAI](https://about.getcoai.com/news/ai-powered-websites-are-impersonating-major-sports-news-outlets); [Futurism](https://futurism.com/google-advon-partnership)
- Raptive: answer-type queries are absorbed by AI Overviews; narrative content less so — [Raptive brief](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2026-03-10-raptive_researchbrief_googlediscovergaming_wp.pdf)

### Inferences
- Writing about living footballers, agents or administrators (match-fixing eras, FAS governance, player conduct) carries real defamation exposure in Singapore; stick to on-record quotes, documented court outcomes, and the subject's own words. Stories about deceased legends and historical events are far safer.
- Image strategy must be decided before launch: own photography at local grounds, Wikimedia Commons/CC, archive partnerships (National Archives, NLB), or paid licences; using Getty/agency match photos on a monetised site invites takedown or claims.
- The single biggest risk is not legal but attrition: documented comparables show two years of heavy output for near-zero income. A sustainable cadence (e.g. one deep story a fortnight plus daily short clips repurposed from it) is more realistic than the daily newsletter model that broke Knell.

### Gaps
- No Singapore-specific case of a sports blogger being sued for defamation found; the precedents are political.
- No current (2025-26) analysis of POFMA application to sports or history content.
- No data on image-licensing costs for small publishers in Singapore.

## Key Question 6: Success cases of solo/small football storytelling creators (2022-2026) and their trajectories

### Takeaway
I found no documented case of a solo football *storytelling* creator who started from zero in 2022-2026 and reached a sustainable income; the only named football-niche comparable that disclosed numbers (Knell) is a cautionary tale. Success stories in the broader space are either older (Ahmed's Goal ~2013, The Underdog Newsletter 2017), team-run (The Gist, Sportskeeda), video-first (Zac Djellab), or non-football (The Pour Over).

## Verdict and conditions (synthesis for the report writer)

### Takeaway
Frank verdict: as described — a text-first independent website plus social page about football stories, started solo in Singapore in late 2026 — this is **not viable as a business in a 1-2 year horizon**, and is marginal at 3+ years. It is viable as a long-horizon passion project with modest side income **only if** it is reshaped around the conditions below.

### Inferences (conditions under which it works)
1. Video-first, website-second: short-form (TikTok/Reels/Shorts) and/or faceless YouTube mini-documentaries are the discovery engine; the website is an archive and email-capture point, not the primary product.
2. Own the audience: an email list from day one (beehiiv/Ghost/Substack); treat every social platform as rented.
3. Differentiate on access, not prose: Singapore and Malaysia Cup history, SEA players, first-person interviews with ex-players and fans, archive material — things AI and global channels cannot replicate. Use global-legend stories only as discovery bait.
4. Sustainable cadence: one deep, primary-sourced story every 1-2 weeks, atomised into daily short clips, rather than daily writing.
5. Legal hygiene: avoid unsourced claims about living people; prefer deceased subjects and documented events; sort image rights (own photos, CC, archive partnerships) before launch.
6. Set expectations: budget 24-36 months to the first meaningful income (sponsorship, small paid tier, a book or events), and decide a kill/continue checkpoint at 12 months based on list size and video retention, not on revenue.
7. Timing: a late-2026 start misses the World Cup 2026 bump; plan content around Asian Cup 2027 (Jan 2027), SPL/Singapore national-team anniversaries, and legend obituaries (pre-written, respectful profiles) for event-driven spikes.
