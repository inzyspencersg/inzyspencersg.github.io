## Key Question 1: What is the repeatable content template (hook, length, arc, visual, ending, CTA)?

### Takeaway
Every durable storytelling brand runs ONE rigid, recognisable unit of content (portrait + first-person quote; one-minute video; 4-8 panel comic; a daily 5-minute newsletter; a Friday letter; a Monday long essay) and repeats it for years. The unit is first-person or intimate in voice, the creator removes themselves from the frame, and the "arc" is usually a single emotional turn (hardship → small resolution) rather than a full narrative.

### Cited Findings

**Humans of New York (photo + first-person text, Facebook/Instagram)**
- HONY began in Nov 2010 as a plan to photograph 10,000 New Yorkers and plot them on a map; Stanton "started adding little quotes and the quotes became interviews, until it grew organically." — [CNBC, Dec 2014](https://www.cnbc.com/2014/12/05/how-ex-bond-traders-humans-of-new-york-became-a-viral-hit.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humans_of_New_York)
- Early posts were brief captions/quotes; later "photo sessions turned into in-depth interviews, often lasting an hour or more," and the work expanded into extensive multi-post series on refugees, federal inmates, etc. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humans_of_New_York); [Pictures and Stories interview, 2018](https://www.picturesandstories.com/news/2018/2/22/the-power-of-listening-an-interview-with-humans-of-new-yorks-brandon-stanton)
- Interview technique: street interviews run 45-60 min; he skips small talk and opens with questions like "What are you most afraid of right now?" or "What's the happiest moment of your life?"; asks one short question at a time; interviews one-on-one because "people are likely to share intimate details about their life if no one else is around"; challenges inconsistencies because "challenging somebody is a form of respect because it shows that you're listening so closely." — [SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/inclusion-diversity/humans-new-york-blogger-shares-interviewing-listening-tips-di-conference); [Medium: Brandon Stanton's Tips for Interviews](https://medium.com/younghumans/brandon-stantons-tips-for-interviews-1a563dd7cb92); [Quirks](https://www.quirks.com/articles/4-things-qualitative-researchers-can-learn-from-humans-of-new-york-founder-brandon-stanton)
- Editing principle: the post is "re-constructing [the interview] in a way that maintains the person's voice and intent, while the photograph maintains their emotion." — [Pictures and Stories, 2018](https://www.picturesandstories.com/news/2018/2/22/the-power-of-listening-an-interview-with-humans-of-new-yorks-brandon-stanton)
- Stanton on why it worked: "I used social media to document stories of people other than myself on a platform where everyone was photographing their food and concerts. And the more I removed myself from the story, the faster it has grown." — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/meet-the-human-behind-humans-of-new-york?amp=1)
- Serialised long-form: the Stephanie "Tanqueray" Johnson story (2020) ran as a multi-part series and the accompanying GoFundMe raised >$2.5M against a $300k goal within ~2 weeks; actress Jennifer Garner commented "Why is this not a @Netflix series?" — [AOL/Yahoo](https://www.aol.com/humans-york-raises-2-6m-213513504.html); [Scary Mommy](https://www.scarymommy.com/humans-of-new-york-tanqueray-donations); [Pamela Grow](https://pamelagrow.com/12829/power-of-storytelling-nearly-1-million-raised-in-three-days)
- 2017 spin-off "Humans of New York: The Series" ran 13 themed episodes ("Time," "Star," "Home") on Facebook Watch. — [Wikipedia: HONY The Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humans_of_New_York:_The_Series)

### Inferences
- The "hook" in these brands is rarely a clever first line; it is the emotional specificity of a real person's first sentence (HONY, Humans of SG) or a shared-identity trigger (TWS's 1997 crisis father; OGS's kopi/ice-cream heritage). For a football storytelling site, the equivalent is a specific, unexpected first-person line from the subject, not editorial framing.
- Length is not the variable; consistency of unit is. Both 60-second (Nas) and 5,000-word (Not Boring) units scaled. What matters is the reader knowing exactly what they get each time.
- Multi-part serialisation (HONY Tanqueray, OGS "Can Ask Meh?", Lenny's mini-series) is the consistent lever for converting casual followers into daily returners.
- "Removing the creator from the story" (Stanton) and "editorially-driven" tone (Rice) are repeated across human-interest brands; the creator's voice shows up in curation and questions, not in the frame.

### Gaps
- No source gave a word-count or panel-count specification for HONY posts or Woke Salaryman comics; exact template lengths would need direct inspection of the pages.
- I found no primary Nas Daily source describing his scripting structure (the Nas Academy course listing promises "the structure behind every viral Nas Daily video" but does not state it).
- Nothing found on Trung Phan or other "fascinating fact" thread accounts beyond Sahil Bloom.

## Key Question 2: Which distribution channel drove the first 10k / 100k / 1M, and what tactics?

### Takeaway
Nearly every brand grew on one borrowed platform first (Facebook for HONY, Humans of SG, OGS, TWS, Nas, Mothership; Twitter for Not Boring, Sahil Bloom, Lenny; campus/word-of-mouth for Morning Brew) and then built an owned channel (email, Patreon, Substack). Documented step-changes were almost always a single external amplification (PM Lee share, Product Hunt launch, NYT bestseller, newsletter cross-recommendations) layered on relentless cadence.

### Inferences
- The "first 10k" is manual and offline/one-to-one in every documented case (campus ambassadors, street interviews, posting daily for 500+ days with zero income). There is no documented shortcut.
- The "100k → 1M" step is driven by network effects native to the platform of the era: Facebook shares (2012-2016), Twitter threads (2020-22), newsletter cross-recommendations and Substack network (2022-25). The channel changes; the pattern (one platform, one unit, daily cadence, borrow others' audiences) does not.
- Referral programs work for email because the reward is cheap and the unit is daily; for a storytelling site the equivalent is a "share this story" mechanic plus recommendation swaps with adjacent newsletters.

### Gaps
- No source gave posting-cadence data for HONY, Humans of SG or TWS (posts per week).
- No documented first-10k story for Mothership, Rice, OGS or Vulcan Post.
- No publisher-level referral data for Threads vs X was found; only platform totals (Threads >400M MAU Aug 2025, 141.5M mobile DAU vs X 125M Jan 2026, but X has 145.4M daily web visits vs Threads 8.5M). — [TechCrunch, Jan 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/18/threads-edges-out-x-in-daily-mobile-users-new-data-shows/); [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/threads-statistics/)

## Key Question 3: How do they structure their websites (archive, categories, search, signup, sharing, SEO)?

### Takeaway
The social-native storytelling brands (HONY, Humans of SG, Nas, OGS) treat the website as a thin brochure/archive and point visitors back to Instagram/Facebook; the text/newsletter brands (Letters of Note, Not Boring, Lenny, TWS) use Substack-style sites where the archive IS the product and the email capture is the primary CTA. Rice and Mothership run conventional ad-supported news sites.

### Cited Findings
- HONY homepage states the latest stories are on Instagram and Facebook; the site presents the project as having interviewed >10,000 people in 40 countries. Series content (refugees, inmates, countries) exists as themed collections. — [humansofnewyork.com](https://humansofnewyork.com); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humans_of_New_York)
- HONY's Patreon page is the de facto membership site ($7/month tier) and hosts long written posts ("The Interview," Apr 2025; "Dear New York"; "Quiet Work"). — [Patreon: humansofnewyork](https://www.patreon.com/humansofnewyork); [Patreon post: The Interview](https://www.patreon.com/posts/interview-125835412)

### Inferences
- For a new storytelling site the two proven architectures are: (a) HONY-style — social feed as the live product, website as archive/about/support page; (b) Substack-style — email as the live product, site as searchable archive with the signup box as the only CTA. Hybrid news-site architecture (Rice/Mothership) requires sales teams and ad volume.
- Given Google referrals fell a third in 2025, SEO should be treated as a long-tail archive benefit, not a growth channel; the archive should still be crawlable (one URL per story, series/tag pages) so that evergreen human-interest pieces accumulate search traffic over years.

- For a Singapore football storytelling site, the defensible mix suggested by the evidence is: free stories → email list → (a) Patreon/paid tier, (b) a small number of clearly labelled brand partnerships with a "we say no a lot" policy, (c) book/zine and live events once the archive is deep.

## Key Question 5: What do founders say about why it worked and what mistakes they made?

### Takeaway
Founders consistently credit (a) removing themselves and centring the subject, (b) obsessive consistency before any income, (c) saying no to misaligned brand work, and (d) word of mouth over paid acquisition. Admitted mistakes cluster around platform dependence, monetising too aggressively (HoB), controversy management (Nas), and lack of a revenue model (Mothership early, Rice late).

## Key Question 6: Evidence on Facebook page reach in 2025-2026 and how storytelling pages adapt

### Takeaway
Facebook organic engagement sits around 0.15% of followers (Socialinsider 2026), link posts are the worst-performing format (~0.05%) and >95% of feed posts carry no external link, so Facebook is a weak traffic driver but still a viable native-reach channel for Reels and image/text posts. Referral traffic to publishers fell ~50% in the year to May 2024 and is still ~43% below May 2023, but was flat-to-up in 2025. Adaptation among storytelling brands is to (1) publish natively on Meta (Reels, carousels, text), (2) optimise for Instagram "sends per reach", (3) build Telegram/Substack/Patreon owned channels, and (4) treat Threads as emerging.

### Cited Findings
- Socialinsider (2026): average Facebook engagement ~0.15%, roughly flat YoY; brands cut posting 22% YoY to ~39 posts/month; Q2 2026 engagement fell across nearly every format — status 0.13%→0.08%, Reels 0.15%→0.13% (still top format), albums 0.11%, images 0.10%, links flat at 0.05%. — [Socialinsider Facebook benchmarks](https://socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/facebook); [Socialinsider Reels](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/facebook-reels-algorithm/)
- Social Status (2024, via secondary): average organic reach 1.37% of followers, median organic engagement 0.2%. — [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/can-i-use-my-facebook-page-instead-of-buying-ads/)
- Rival IQ 2025: engagement down 36% on Facebook, 16% Instagram, 34% TikTok YoY (median-based method, not comparable to Socialinsider averages); Instagram all-industry median ~0.30-0.36%; sports teams the highest Facebook vertical at 0.23%. — [Rival IQ 2025 benchmark](https://www.rivaliq.com/blog/social-media-industry-benchmark-report/); [Bigeye](https://www.bigeyeagency.com/insights/social-media-engagement-rate-benchmarks-by-industry)
- Links: Meta says >95% of feed posts carry no external link and the share keeps falling; Facebook has not said it directly penalises links. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/heres-where-each-of-the-big-social-platform-stands-on-external-links/733946/)

### Inferences
- For a new Singapore page in 2026, Facebook remains relevant because the Singapore audience is older/web-first and Mothership-style native posting still reaches 45% of the population weekly — but expect ~0.1-0.2% engagement per post and near-zero link clicks. Post the full story natively (text + photo, or Reel) and keep the link in comments/first-comment or bio.
- Instagram carousels (photo + first-person text slides) map directly onto the HONY unit and are optimised for "sends" (people DM a touching story to a friend) — likely the best cold-reach channel for human-interest in 2026.
- Telegram is an under-used owned channel in Singapore with proven uptake (Mothership 247k); pair with email.
- TikTok offers the highest tier-mobility for a zero-follower start, but Nas's revenue data shows it pays nothing directly; it is a top-of-funnel tool only.

## Synthesis: Common patterns (for the report writer)

1. One rigid unit, repeated for years (portrait+quote; 1-min video; comic; Friday letter; Monday essay). Sources: HONY, Nas, TWS, LoN, Not Boring (cited above).
2. Subject-first, creator-invisible voice; the creator shows up only in question quality and curation (Stanton; Rice's "editorially-driven"; LoN's archival sourcing).
3. First 10k is manual and unpaid (campus ambassadors; 500 videos with zero income; 2010-2013 Facebook grind).
4. One platform at a time, then an owned layer (Facebook→Patreon; Twitter→Substack; email→referral program→events).
5. Step-changes come from external amplification of a single emotionally specific story (PM Lee share; Tanqueray; Product Hunt; newsletter recommendations), not from optimisation.
6. Serialisation and recurring sub-formats convert followers to habit (HONY series, "Can Ask Meh?", Lenny mini-series, Brew's Sunday edition).
7. Monetise off-platform: patronage, labelled brand partnerships with a strict refusal policy, paid email (~4-5% conversion benchmark), books, live events, courses. Platform ad share is negligible (606M views → $33k).
8. Branded-content-only Singapore publishers (Rice) proved fragile; social-enterprise/no-revenue starts (Mothership) survived only by building content-marketing services; the comic duo (TWS) and documentary studio (OGS) survived on brand work plus diversified channels.
9. In 2025-26, Facebook is a native-reach channel not a traffic channel (~0.15% engagement; links ~0.05%; referrals -43% vs 2023); Instagram rewards shares/sends; Telegram and Substack are the owned channels Singapore brands actually use; Threads is emerging but sends little web traffic.
