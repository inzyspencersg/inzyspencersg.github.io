# The Morning Founder (themorningfounder.com) and Joel Chue — profile for replication in a football niche

**Research date:** 18 October 2026. **Access note:** Direct fetches of themorningfounder.com, the beehiiv mirror, LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram and web.archive.org were all blocked from this research environment (proxy 403 / DNS refusal). Every finding below comes from search-engine snippets of the site's own pages and of Joel Chue's LinkedIn profile, so quotes are partial and page-level details (prices, live subscriber counts, exact layout) could not be verified. Treat all figures as "as snippet-indexed, mid-2026".

**Important premise correction (confirmed by multiple snippets):** The Morning Founder is NOT a Facebook-first "Humans of Singapore"-style storytelling page about living people interviewed by the author. It is a free, daily, *email-newsletter-first* publication of researched Southeast Asian business-history stories (one founder/company per issue, 1,200–1,800 words, with a commissioned watercolour illustration), with a companion website that republishes stories a week later. No official Facebook or Instagram page for it could be found at all; the only social channel with evidence of use is Joel Chue's personal LinkedIn profile. The "storytelling page that grew on Facebook" framing in the brief does not match what the evidence shows. The repeatable formula is still highly transferable to football (see Inferences), but the growth engine is newsletter + LinkedIn + paid ads, not Facebook shares.

### Takeaway
A one-person, free daily newsletter and website by Singapore-based former performance marketer Joel Chue, publishing since 2024, that retells the stories of Southeast Asian founders and companies ("the people who built Southeast Asia, and the decisions that made it work"), published through a Singapore company, ReallyCreativeIdeas.com Pte Ltd.

Lau Award" in 2023 for community work unrelated to the newsletter; grew up in Malaysia and Singapore and started writing about brands from that background — [About — The Morning Founder](https://themorningfounder.com/about/) (snippet; the award and press mentions are the site's own claims and could not be independently verified — see Gaps)
- Publisher: ReallyCreativeIdeas.com Pte Ltd, 45B Temple Street, Singapore 058590 — [Terms](https://themorningfounder.com/terms/)
- Joel Chue's LinkedIn headline reads "Joel Chue - TheMorningFounder.com"; profile describes 20+ years in performance-based marketing, NUS education, Singapore, 500+ connections — [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/joel-chue-aa698a51/)

## Key Question 2: What exactly is the repeatable story template (format, length, structure, tone, headline, imagery, cadence, platforms)?

### Takeaway
One founder/company per issue, 1,200–1,800 words (about a two-minute "watercolour" read on the site), built from public records with a double-source fact-check, framed around a single decision "that looked wrong at the time", headlined as "Company / Founder name", paired with one commissioned watercolour, numbered as a "Plate", emailed every morning (8am SGT weekdays, 10am weekends), and republished on the website one week later.

### Cited Findings
- Length and cadence: "Each issue is one company's story, not news or a roundup. One goes out every morning, seven days a week, at 1,200 to 1,800 words, with a watercolour made for it" — [homepage](https://www.themorningfounder.com/)
- Delivery times: "Every morning, 8am Singapore time on weekdays, 10am at weekends" — [Subscribe](https://themorningfounder.com/subscribe)
- Website lag: "stories appear on the site a week after subscribers read them"; some story pages say "the newsletter gets the complete text first" (newer stories appear in full only in the newsletter) — [Archive](https://themorningfounder.com/stories); [NousQ story](https://themorningfounder.com/stories/nousq)
- Headline convention: "Company / Founder — The Morning Founder", e.g. "Vodien / Alvin Poh", "Carousell / Quek Siu Rui", "Sriracha / David Tran", "SQ117 / Fred Cheong", "NKF / Gerard Ee", "Christina Ong / Club 21 / COMO Group", "Calbee / Takashi Matsuo", "NousQ / CLiKX", or just a person's name ("Hugh Harrison", "Alec Fraser-Brunner") — [Archive search results](https://themorningfounder.com/stories)
- Each story is numbered as a "Plate" (e.g. "Plate 075" for Vodien, "Plate 120", "Plate 148"), tagged by country (e.g. "Singapore") and category (e.g. "Food & drink"), labelled "Watercolour" format, and shown with a reading time ("about a two-minute read") — [Archive](https://themorningfounder.com/stories); [Calbee story](https://www.themorningfounder.com/stories/calbee)
- Illustration: "each story is paired with a watercolour made for it" — [homepage](https://www.themorningfounder.com/)
- Teaser/hook style (archive blurbs): Vodien — built the business around 24/7 phone support he could only staff himself; sold in 2017 for $30 million at 33. Carousell — began as a Startup Weekend pitch that was initially rejected; founders later turned down US$100 million. Sriracha — built on chili sauce with no marketing spend. NKF — an accountant brought in to fix the scandal after retirement. Club 21 — began as a single luxury fashion shop in 1972. Hugh Harrison — the foreign-born writer of "Count On Me, Singapore". NousQ — a Singapore surgeon's handheld ear-tube device — [Archive](https://themorningfounder.com/stories)
- Editorial angle: the About page describes a research brief of "about ten to twenty pages for each story" and a focus on "decisions that looked wrong at the time" — [About](https://themorningfounder.com/about/)
- Research/fact-check method: stories are "rebuilt from public material such as company filings, news reports, interviews, books, and archives"; "each claim is checked twice against at least two sources, with names, dates, figures, and superlatives checked most closely"; where sources disagree, the story says so rather than picking one; errors go on a dated corrections page; readers with first-hand knowledge are asked to report mistakes — [Disclaimer](https://themorningfounder.com/disclaimer/); [About](https://themorningfounder.com/about/); [Terms](https://themorningfounder.com/terms/)
- Example story content (Calbee): wartime work turning food waste into dumplings → founding in Hiroshima 1949 → 1964 Kappa Ebisen launch → framing that the name (calcium + B1) reflects the mission — [Calbee story](https://www.themorningfounder.com/stories/calbee)
- Example story content (Fraser-Brunner, dated 18 July 2026): fish scientist who designed the Merlion; British Museum, Edinburgh Zoo aquarium, first curator of Van Kleef Aquarium; flags an unconfirmed "story passed down" that he started as a commercial artist — [Fraser-Brunner story](https://themorningfounder.com/stories/fraser-brunner-merlion/)
- Subject scope: "from Singapore, Malaysia and beyond" — including non-SEA subjects (Calbee/Japan, Sriracha/US) and non-business "founders" of national symbols (Merlion, "Count On Me, Singapore") — [Archive](https://themorningfounder.com/stories)
- LinkedIn repurposing format: Chue's recent LinkedIn posts "open with a founder story and close with a question for readers" (e.g. a banker who built a patient bank in Asia; a doctor who coached a family of swimmers) — [LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/in/joel-chue-aa698a51/)

### Inferences
- The template, reconstructed from snippets: (1) a Company/Person slug headline; (2) a one-line "hook" teaser stating the counter-intuitive decision or a striking number (refunded every customer; turned down US$100m; sold for $30m at 33); (3) a chronological narrative of 1,200–1,800 words built around one decision that "looked wrong at the time"; (4) explicit handling of disputed facts in-text; (5) one commissioned watercolour as the only image (no photos — avoids rights issues with historical subjects); (6) numbered "Plate" to signal a collectible series; (7) a mid-story sponsor slot; (8) CTA to subscribe (website) or share/reply (newsletter — inferred). The ending/CTA of the email version could not be read directly.
- The "Plate" + watercolour + "from the record" vocabulary is a deliberate heritage/archival aesthetic (the term "plate" comes from book illustration — [Wikipedia: Tipped-in page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tipped-in_page)); for a football version the analogue would be a consistent illustrated "card"/"plate" per player or match rather than licensed photos.
- Sourcing is desk research, not interviews or reader nominations. No nomination/submission mechanism was found; the only reader input requested is corrections.

### Gaps
- Could not read a full story page, so the exact paragraph structure, closing line, in-email CTA, share buttons and image credits are unverified.
- Who paints the watercolours (Chue, a commissioned illustrator, or AI-generated) is not stated in any snippet.
- Tone can only be inferred from blurbs (plain, declarative, number-led, understated); no direct long passage was retrievable.

### Takeaway

Growth evidence points to email + LinkedIn organic posting + paid ads (a performance marketer's playbook), not Facebook. There is no sign of press coverage.

### Cited Findings

- Archive growth markers: Subscribe/Archive pages (older snapshot, archive page "about 75 days old" per the search engine) say "120 stories"; homepage says "156 plates" / "156 in the archive and one more every morning" / "144 others in the archive"; About says "roughly 260 more are researched and waiting" — [Subscribe](https://themorningfounder.com/subscribe); [Archive](https://themorningfounder.com/stories); [homepage](https://www.themorningfounder.com/); [About](https://themorningfounder.com/about/)

A follow-up search indicated this text appears in the "Explore more posts" section of his profile page and most likely belongs to a different author (Olusegun Joe-Alabi, MingoolAI). ## Key Question 5: What is the business model?

### Takeaway
Free newsletter monetised by per-issue sponsorship, sold directly by email, with two ad units (a 70-word in-story "Primary" and a 25-word logo "Frontispiece"), strict editorial separation, and live beehiiv metrics as the pitch. No paid tier, events, merchandise or agency services were found.

### Cited Findings
- Newsletter is free — [Terms](https://themorningfounder.com/terms/)
- Ad units per issue: "Primary" = 70-word slot placed inside the story (partway through, not at the bottom), one link, optional 600×600 image, copy written by advertiser or drafted in the newsletter's voice for approval; "Frontispiece" = 25-word logo-only slot under the header, one link, up to two per issue. "One primary placement per issue." Booking by emailing the issue dates wanted. Subscriber count and 30-day open rate shown live from beehiiv. No price shown in indexed text — [Sponsor an issue](https://themorningfounder.com/advertise)
- Terms: sponsors buy "a labelled slot in an issue, not editorial control"; a company previously covered can sponsor but not in the same issue, and that issue is labelled — [Terms](https://themorningfounder.com/terms/)
- Disclaimer: content is not financial/business/legal advice; publisher is "not affiliated with, endorsed by, sponsored by or authorised by any company, founder, family or estate written about" — [Disclaimer](https://themorningfounder.com/disclaimer/)
- Publisher entity: ReallyCreativeIdeas.com Pte Ltd — [Terms](https://themorningfounder.com/terms/)

### Inferences
- This is the classic "Morning Brew for X" sponsorship model (free daily email, sold per-send) applied to a history niche; the naming ("The Morning Founder") itself echoes Morning Brew.
- The 260-story research backlog is a content moat that lets a solo operator guarantee daily cadence, which is what makes per-issue sponsorship sellable.
- Pricing is likely quoted privately (inference; not found).

### Gaps
- Sponsorship rates, number of sponsors to date, and any revenue are unknown.
- No evidence either way on future paid tiers, books/compilations ("plates" would lend themselves to a print book — inference only), or events.

## Key Question 6: What lessons has Joel Chue publicly shared about growing the page (his "playbook")?

### Takeaway
Very little. No interview, podcast or "lessons learned" post by Chue about The Morning Founder was found. The only first-person statements are on the site itself (not a journalist; research-from-the-record; one decision per story; daily no matter what) and in LinkedIn posts that use the story-then-question format. Earlier LinkedIn posts from his marketing career emphasise consistency and Pareto focus.

### Cited Findings
- First-person positioning on About: not a journalist; twenty years in performance marketing; built and sold an app business; grew up in Malaysia and Singapore and "started writing about brands from that background" — [About](https://themorningfounder.com/about/)
- Method statements: ten-to-twenty-page research brief per story; decisions "that looked wrong at the time"; every claim checked twice against two sources; disagreements stated in the text — [About](https://themorningfounder.com/about/); [Disclaimer](https://themorningfounder.com/disclaimer/)
- Growth anecdote: first (sambal) story read by ~4,000 people, which he describes as far above expectations — [homepage](https://www.themorningfounder.com/)
- Distribution habit: LinkedIn posts that open with a founder story and close with a question for readers — [LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/in/joel-chue-aa698a51/)
- Older LinkedIn themes (pre-newsletter, 2022): "conversion rates, Pareto-style focus, and consistency"; "Dear Singaporeans, to escape the rat race, you need to be a producer…" — [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/joel-chue-aa698a51_dear-singaporeans-to-escape-the-rat-race-activity-6996289817776922624-fDgV); [Clickbank post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/joel-chue-aa698a51_my-clickbank-plaque-finally-arrived-today-activity-6959837402760691712-ocRf)

### Inferences (the implied playbook, for a football adaptation)
- Pick a tight, repeatable unit: one person/club + one decision, 1,200–1,800 words, every single day at a fixed time. Build a large researched backlog (he claims ~260) before/while launching so cadence never breaks.
- Lead with the counter-intuitive decision or a single striking number as the hook (e.g. for football: "turned down a £100m move", "refunded every season-ticket holder").
- Use a consistent, rights-free visual identity (one commissioned illustration per story, numbered as a collectible series) instead of licensed photos — directly relevant for football where match photography rights are expensive.
- Make the email the product: website publishes a week later and never the complete text, so the archive drives subscriptions.
- Repurpose each story as a LinkedIn (or, for football, X/Threads/Facebook) post: story in the first lines, question at the end to drive comments.
- Monetise from day one with transparent, labelled, per-issue sponsorship and live audience metrics; keep an explicit editorial-independence policy and a corrections page to build trust in historical claims.
- Be explicit about not being a journalist and about method (two sources per claim) — turns the amateur status into a credibility signal.

### Gaps
- No direct quotes from Chue on what made it grow, which channels worked, or subscriber milestones. If the user can open LinkedIn directly, his activity feed (linkedin.com/in/joel-chue-aa698a51) is the most likely place such statements exist.
- No podcast or press interview exists in the indexed web as of Oct 2026.

## Comparison notes (Singapore storytelling pages mentioned in the brief)
- The brief suggested comparing with Humans of Singapore / Our Grandfather Story. The Morning Founder does not reference them, and its model differs fundamentally (desk-researched historical narratives via email vs. interview-based portraits on Facebook/video). Humans of Singapore was started by SMU graduate Shitij Nigam, inspired by Humans of New York — [SMU news](https://news.smu.edu.sg/news/2014/08/11/they-found-themselves-their-new-homes). No comparative follower data was gathered since it was outside what the Morning Founder evidence supports.
- Closest structural analogue is the "Morning Brew for X" newsletter model; Southeast Asian examples with published growth stories include BackScoop (Amanda Cua, Philippines, 10,000+ active subscribers) — [Inbox Collective](https://inboxcollective.com/how-amanda-cua-built-backscoop-southeast-asias-go-to-tech-newsletter/) — which the user may find more useful as a documented playbook than Chue, who has published none.
